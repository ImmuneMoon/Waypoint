WAYPOINT 1.5.0 — What's New
============================

Play map & settings
- Drawing: a Flat (chisel) pen now shows its true angled ribbon as you draw,
  matching the finished stroke.
- Fit to Grid Cells greys out (and says "Already aligned to the grid") when
  the selection already sits on the grid, so it only lights up when it would
  move something.
- The grid button has a ◑ shortcut straight to the grid-opacity setting.
- Undo history size is now adjustable — Settings ▸ Advanced, 10–500 steps
  (default 100) — with a note on its memory cost; and a few heavy features
  (fog, minimap, sound) carry a short hardware-impact note in Settings.
- Selecting on the play map: a single click selects and a double-click opens
  Properties (text boxes still edit and portals still travel on a
  double-click); Ctrl-click adds to or takes from a selection; a click on a
  token under a label, note or backdrop takes the token; and a drag that
  starts on a locked item, such as a background, sweeps a selection box. An
  item you fitted with Fit to Grid Cells stays seated on the grid whenever
  you move it, Snap or not.
- A shorter play-map toolbar: Text box, Image, Image Library and Import
  Character sit under + Add, and Sound, Music and Visual effects under
  🎬 Scene; Draw, Eraser and Fill form one group. Only one toolbar menu is
  open at a time, clicking the active tool while its menu is open puts it
  away and returns to the arrow, and the Sound and Visual effects panels
  take turns instead of stacking. An open toolbar menu, such as the fog
  menu, draws over a selected token's toolbar instead of under it.
- The Planners, Handbook and Maps headers in the sidebar show just their
  title: right-click one for its actions (collapse all, search and new, plus
  new from a Markdown file for planners and pages).
- Item badges (the hidden eye, the sight-blocker and door marks, the portal
  icon, the elevation and posture chips) keep their size on screen at any
  zoom, so they stay legible zoomed out. Zooming in past about 2× no longer
  throws the view somewhere else: it stays under the pointer, on the data
  map and the play map.
- Hover cards stay inside the board at every edge, and a character's card
  shows its portrait (the token's picture or face) and shortens long notes,
  which no longer read in capitals. The party and combat strips show one
  tooltip, not two.

Tokens and portals
- Tokens come in the grid's shape: a token the app places (a player's
  waiting or new token, one arriving through a portal, a summon or Bring, a
  roster, cast or imported character) takes the shape of the map's cells, a
  hexagon on a hex map, a square on a square map and a circle only with no
  grid, its picture cut to match. Tokens already on a map, and tokens you
  sized up, keep their shape; Fit to Grid Cells gives a one-cell token the
  cell's shape in one press.
- The token creator: your profile picture, a character's portrait
  (Portrait…, a room character's, a ShadowBase import) and a picture a
  player uploads for their character or their own token open it first —
  drag to move, scroll, pinch or the slider to zoom, and OK keeps what is
  inside the square (untouched: the middle, as before). One square picture
  is saved; each map's grid gives it its outline, and the Hexagon, Square
  and Circle chips preview them. Frame picture… on a picture token's menu
  frames any token (the selected plain ones, or all of a character's);
  with Keep the original, ticked for you, you can zoom back out later, and
  your players never get the original. A player's own picture keeps none:
  they choose a picture again.
- A new picture always gets a new file name, so everyone sees the latest at
  once, and a character's or a token's older pictures are cleared from your
  saves folder (the one before stays, and anything still in use).
- Token facing is a VTT feature (Settings ▸ VTT features), on by default and
  yours to set for the table, like fog: switched off, tokens lose their
  facing arrow (and character tokens their rotate handle), and a map set to
  a facing cone sees all around instead.
- A locked token is frozen for its player too: they can no longer move or
  turn it, change its posture or elevation, or mark threats on it until you
  unlock it, and their app stops offering to.
- GM note / dialogue, in a character token's Properties, holds private notes
  and lines to read aloud for that one token. It shows on your hover card
  under the stats and never reaches players.
- A token's Properties show the Info and Reference of the data-map character
  it is linked to, and editing them there edits that character (with a note
  when several tokens share it). An unlinked token offers Link to a
  character…: one of the campaign's characters, or a new one named after the
  token.
- Trap tiles: a hidden portal does nothing, as before, until you tick
  "⚠️ Trap — fires while hidden" in its Properties. Players never see the
  tile, but a token that lands on it is teleported; you see a red ⚠️ and a
  dashed outline, and a locked destination map still stops it.
- On a player's screen, a picture token whose picture is still arriving
  shows the character's initials in a dashed circle instead of an empty box.

Welcome screen, profile and tour
- On a fresh Waypoint the tour starts once you are past the welcome screen
  (Start a campaign or Enter Waypoint), before anything is made: the
  Tutorial campaign is your first unless you skip the tour, and the prompt
  naming a campaign of your own follows the tour or its Skip. The welcome
  lists no campaign to continue until one holds something.
- Waypoint opens on a Welcome screen: Start a campaign, Import one from a
  file, Continue one already on this computer, or Join a game. The Waypoint
  logo at the top left brings it back any time, and Settings ▸ Profile ▸
  Welcome screen on launch sets how often it appears (every time, first run
  only or never).
- Join a game has a screen of its own: your name, colour, picture and token
  face, the room code and a password if the GM set one. It shows the
  connection as it goes (connecting, waiting for the GM to let you in, or
  what went wrong) and takes you to the table once the GM's campaign
  arrives; ← Back abandons the join.
- Your profile (name, colour and picture) is kept on this computer and goes
  with you to every table; set it on the Join screen or in Settings ▸
  Profile. A name is required to host or join. A new profile starts on a
  random colour, so players are told apart at once, and anyone without a
  picture shows a silhouette on their own colour (in the roster, on the
  party strip and on the Maps list) instead of a "?" or initials; so does a
  character's hover card when it has no picture.
- The tour is grouped into seven parts you can jump between (the ▾ menu on
  each card, or Help ▸ Tutorial), and each card shows how far you are
  through its part, with a slim bar that fills part by part, instead of one
  long count through the whole tour.

Players and their characters
- A player now follows their character, not its name, so renaming a
  character or its tokens no longer loses them, and plays one character at a
  time. Give a character… on their chip on the party strip (or a token's
  Player Owner, or In play in System ▸ Characters) puts one into play, its
  token on the map they are on; their others are kept (sheets and rolls
  still theirs, switched from the picker at the top of the sheet) while you
  move those tokens. The Players panel reads "plays Brakka · also Wolf", and
  the Session Log records each change.
- A player who joins with no character gets a waiting token: their face in a
  dashed gold outline that they can move about the map they are on, until
  you give them a character and their real token takes its place. It follows
  them from map to map, goes three minutes after they leave, and is never
  saved; right-click it, or their chip, for Give a character…, Hide or
  Remove. A card tells them where they stand.
- Each player chooses their token's face (their picture, the plain default,
  a bundled token picture or an emoji) on the Join screen, in Settings ▸
  Profile ▸ Token face or from their card. Their chip wears it too, and a
  change reaches the table at once.
- A character you give a player at the table, with no picture of its own,
  takes a copy of their face and keeps it: a later profile change never
  touches it (untick Use their face at the top of the Give list to leave it
  bare). The player changes it with Picture on their own sheet (a face, a
  bundled picture or an upload) and you are told. Its tokens and chip wear
  the picture.
- With character sheets on, a player can make their own: Make a character…
  on their card (or My character… on their chip) starts a sheet whose every
  visible field is theirs to fill in, seen by no other player until they
  press Done. It then goes into play at once, in their waiting token's place
  (unless you gave them a character meanwhile), and you are told. Its sheet
  shows you a review bar until you press Keep it: Send back… unlocks it for
  them with your note, Remove… deletes it with its tokens. Unlock on any
  player's sheet lets them fill in every field they can see; Lock ends it.
- Just a token… on their card is the quick way in: a name and their face,
  and they play at once (with character sheets, an empty character they can
  fill in later, which comes to your review like one they made; without, a
  plain token of theirs).
- A player making a character can start it from a file: Start from a file…
  on their card, or Import on its sheet, takes their own sheet download
  (.wpchar.json) or a ShadowBase sheet and fills it at once with their own
  rights, its skills and gear linked to the library packs they can see or
  else rows of their own, and tells them what did not come across. You
  review the whole character when they press Done; untick …may start it
  from a file in the 🌐 Multiplayer window to turn it off.
- Players without a character, in the 🌐 Multiplayer window, turns waiting
  tokens off for a campaign, can let them see around themselves under fog,
  and sets when players may make a character of their own: at once, only
  when you ask (Let them make a character on their chip; for a player who
  already plays one, it is a replacement) or never. Forget, under Player
  History & Bans, now asks first, holds until you next let them in, and
  removes a character they were still making.

Running the table
- Pause this player, on a player's token or their chip on the party strip,
  freezes just that player while the rest play on: their chip wears a blue
  ring and they see a banner, with chat still open. A ⏸️ button in the top
  Table bar, shown while you host, pauses the whole table from either map,
  in step with the 🌐 panel and the right-click menu.
- While you view a map other than the table's, the party strip's right-click
  menu also offers Summon … to this map: for one player (a scout ahead; the
  rest stay put) or for everyone (the whole table moves here, and it becomes
  the table's map). The table's-map entries name that map, so the two are
  never confused.
- The Maps list shows small faces for the players on each map, solid for
  anyone there now and dimmed where a player was last seen, so a split party
  is plain at a glance. Only you see them.
- A player's top bar names the campaign and, in a section of its own, the
  map they are on. It follows them through doors, travel and summons, and
  picks up a campaign or map renamed mid-session.
- The combat roster sends players the turn order, never an initiative
  number, and a Hidden row carries nothing of its creature. Every row of
  your roster now shows its name (the initiative box had squeezed the names
  out).

Protecting your table
- Your table key is never sent again. A player your table already knows is
  asked to prove the key over a one-time challenge bound to the very room
  they dialled, so a key cannot be collected by another host, by someone
  squatting a later room code, or on the wire. A player whose key does not
  match (a fresh install, or someone else using that name) is put to you
  for Allow/Deny as before. A player's app answers one challenge per
  connection, keeps a key only from the host it joined, and holds at most
  the 50 newest tables.
- A player's app holds only the map they are on. The map they land on, and
  each one they travel or are summoned to, arrives whole the moment they
  do, and the map they leave is taken back: their copy goes back to its
  name and its lock, nothing you change there afterwards reaches them, and
  the whole map is sent again when they return. Every other map of your
  campaign is a name in their list and nothing more, so a locked map, one
  the party has not found, or one they walked out of carries no tokens,
  labels or room names on their machine.
- Your players' apps know your table from anyone else's. Your app signs the
  table it hands each player, and their apps remember your key the first
  time they join. If someone else answers at your room code while you are
  away, a reconnecting player is not shown that table: their app says so
  once and keeps looking for you. A player who presses Join and finds a
  different GM there is asked once whether to trust that table; say yes
  only if your GM tells you they reinstalled or changed computers. This
  begins with the first table hosted on this version; a table hosted on an
  older version joins as before.
- Reset Multiplayer Identity also gives your table a new signing key (to
  your players' apps it is then a new GM's table), and Cancel on its
  question now really cancels: it used to reset your identity either way.
- Your room code is now ten characters, shown as XXXXX-XXXXX. Copy copies
  it that way and your players can type it with or without the hyphen. It
  is much harder to guess, and Help says plainly what a code gives away:
  on a direct connection whoever dials it can see your network address;
  with Firewall-friendly connections and a relay they see only the relay's.
- If someone else takes your room code while your link to the matchmaking
  service blinks, your table stays up: your players stay connected, you
  are told once, and Waypoint takes the code back by itself when it is
  free. Before, your own app ended the table and moved to a new code.
- Firewall-friendly connections tells you the truth: with no relay set on
  this computer it says it is connecting directly instead of claiming
  relay-only, the switch stays in view so you can turn it off, and
  removing the relay turns it off with it.
- A returning player's key check is tied to the encrypted connection
  itself. If their answer was made over a different connection you are
  asked, in words that say so, instead of the player going straight in.
- Someone who only knows your room code can no longer make your app hold
  data without limit, flood it with connection attempts or cover your
  screen with error banners. A player whose app stops keeping up no longer
  makes yours queue everything for them: they are dropped and rejoin by
  themselves.
- The stream window shows the party's view: on a fogged map its fog is the
  union of what your players' own tokens see, creatures none of them sees
  are left out, and its Focus on menu lists players' characters alone.
- A player's map changes land only on the hosted campaign and only on the
  map they are on; their drawings are bounded by points as well as count
  (60,000 a map), a new drawing's id is plain text, and a double-click on a
  door no longer carries a token across the map — the token standing on the
  door steps through, one your fight or your lock holds stays, with a word
  why.
- A player's app is served only the pictures and sounds it was sent a
  reference to, never a Journal or video file, and nothing past the size
  cap is read at all. Before you let a player in, their app sends nothing
  of its own campaign to you, and a handout or notepad is taken only from
  the host that admitted them, filed under that table alone. What players
  share has a budget of its own, apart from your handouts, and a shared
  page is replaced only by its own sender.
- Importing a campaign file never replaces a file already in your saves
  folder (the import says how many were already here) and never writes a
  Journal; the players a file names come in without their keys, so the
  file's author is asked at your table like anyone new.
- A yes you give to a joiner whose connection has dropped admits nobody by
  that name later: you are asked again when they come back. One Start-combat
  offer is open per player at a time. A wrong session password locks out
  only the name it was tried under, for a minute, never the whole table. At
  most 32 connections wait for admission at once, and a stranger's dropped
  connection costs you no save and no roster message. Each player's app is
  served at most 150 MB of pictures and sounds a minute.
- A player's app takes an "end" only from its own host, keeps a host's
  positions only as sane numbers, refuses any message past the app's own
  sizes, shows a refusal in its own words (never the host's text, never a
  dialog the host can raise again), and draws a flood of chat lines once.
- Smaller leaks closed: an unseen creature's initiative roll stays private,
  a trigger zone's message reaches a player only when their own token
  enters it, the party strip's hover names only effects a teammate may see,
  and your other campaigns are never named to a table.
Updates
- Updates are signed. Your copy takes an update only when its signature
  checks against a key that only Waypoint's maker holds; an update that is
  not signed, or was changed after it was signed, is refused and your copy
  stays exactly as it was.
- Restore the previous version. After an update, Settings ▸ Updates &
  about shows a button that puts the app back as it was before that
  update. Your campaigns are not touched, and you can update again
  afterwards.
- An update that changes Waypoint's core is one click too. You no longer
  download and run the installer yourself: press Update and Waypoint
  downloads the new version, checks it, saves your campaign, closes,
  updates itself and opens again. If that cannot be done on your copy, Get
  Installer still shows the steps, and if a core update is started and
  does not finish, Waypoint tells you at the next launch and offers both
  ways on.
- Coming from 1.4.9 or earlier, you install 1.5.0 with the installer one
  last time; from then on every update installs itself.
- The installer dialog no longer asks you to click past the Windows
  warning. It says plainly that the installer is not code-signed yet, and
  to run only a Waypoint_Setup.exe you got through the app's own button or
  from Waypoint's releases page.
Character sheets: the system
- Character sheets start with the system: a System button in the Campaign
  pill opens an editor for the campaign's rules, with no code. Fields with a
  key formulas use (STR, Skill.Stealth), a label and a kind: numbers with a
  default and range, formulas computed from other fields
  (floor((STR - 10) / 2)), resources with a formula for their max (HP),
  skills as ranks plus a base, toggles for conditions, text, notes and
  selects. Named rolls with dice (d20 + STRmod, 3d6 <= Skill.Stealth) become
  buttons on the sheet. Errors show under a field as you type, with a caret;
  loops and dice inside definitions are refused; Compute with defaults shows
  every number. Start from Basic d20 or Basic 3d6, or blank; Export and
  Import move a system between campaigns as a .wpsystem.json file.
- Each field is visible to players or GM only (a GM-only field never leaves
  your machine), editable by the player or by the GM, and can appear on the
  hover card. The visible part of the system reaches players at your table
  at once. Character sheets is a VTT feature (Settings ▸ VTT features).
- Characters: the editor's Characters tab creates the campaign's characters
  (a player, NPC, a portrait); a token points at one through its Properties
  or right-click ▸ New character sheet, and any number of tokens can share a
  character (one HP across maps). The character's player is written onto its
  tokens, so moves and the party strip work as before. Right-click a token ▸
  Sheet… opens the sheet over the play map: numbers and skills with totals,
  resources as a bar with − and +, toggles, text, notes, selects, formulas
  read-only; every change saves at once, ↶ puts the last one back.
- Players open their own sheet the same way and fill in what you left
  editable; your machine checks every value and keeps it. Of other PCs they
  see only the Hover fields, on the hover card and the party strip; NPCs
  never leave your machine.
- Rolls from the sheet: the roll buttons on a sheet (and a field's own die
  beside its label) roll at the table like /roll, with the character's
  values filled in; the card shows the label, who rolled as whom, and every
  name with the value it used. The roller gains a character picker (your own
  characters; the GM any), so /roll d20 + STR works once a character is
  picked; a GM's roll that uses a GM-only value stays private. In the combat
  roster, a die beside a row rolls initiative from the system's Initiative
  roll.
- Layout: the editor's Layout tab arranges the sheet in sections of 1–4
  columns — fields, roll buttons, headings, dividers and the portrait, each
  one column or the full row, dragged between sections — with a live preview
  for a chosen character. Without a layout the sheet arranges itself by
  kind; Start from the automatic layout copies that.
- Bridges: a token with a ShadowBase sheet attached gets Character from this
  sheet (a copy of its attributes, resources and skills by the system's
  matching keys, and much more on a system built for it: see ShadowBase
  sheets); the party strip's right-click menu offers Sheet… and Characters…;
  the Tutorial campaign carries a small system with Bren's sheet, and the
  tour opens it.
- GM-only values stay yours: your roll that reads a value worked out from a
  GM-only field (a visible formula built on it, a skill whose base reads
  it), or whose label shows one, stays private, as do a GM-only field's own
  roll, a GM-only roll and the damage of a GM-only item, whose blast reaches
  players without its name. A resource whose maximum reads a GM-only field
  is GM only as a whole, and a skill whose base reads one shows players
  "GM only" rather than a wrong total.
- Importing a system file tells you what it left out (items over the limits,
  a malformed field, an effect that did not read), counted by kind: "Left
  out (over the limits, or not valid): 5 items".

Character sheets: building the sheet
- Tabs and sections: add Tabs at the top of the Layout tab and give each
  section a tab, and the sheet shows a tab strip; with none it stays one
  stacked page. A section can be Collapsible (the reader folds it away),
  show a Count in its header (any field's value, such as a points total),
  sit under another as a sub-section, and wear its own colours (an accent, a
  panel and a border; untick Stripe to colour only its title). A sheet holds
  up to 40 sections.
- The top of the sheet: Identity rows (a small label over each value) and
  Ledger figures (a label over a bold number, red when negative) make a
  header block under the name that scrolls away with the sheet; text,
  select, number and yes/no rows and ledger numbers are changed right where
  they are read. The Pinned band keeps a few numbers, resources, toggles or
  roll buttons in reach on every tab, staying at the top with the tab strip
  while the sheet scrolls; a section set Above the tabs is a dashboard on
  every tab.
- Pin groups: put band figures in named groups (Points, Spell slots) and
  give each a Pin button where its figures live. Each viewer pins the groups
  they want onto the band, remembered on their own machine for each
  character; figures in no group always show.
- Fields: Tile shows a number, formula, skill or resource as a boxed stat
  tile for an ability-score grid; Slider shows a number with a min and a max
  on a two-colour track with end labels (an alignment, a mood), still a
  plain number to formulas. A value can carry a Unit (pts, kg) and ± colour
  (green above zero, red below). A resource can take an icon, a ↻ button
  that fills it to its max, and no bar.
- Captions and value names: any field can take a Caption, a line under it
  with values worked out for the character ("Base: {ST * 2}"; {±X} adds the
  sign, +2), and a roll's label can show values the same way
  ("Attack ({±AtkBonus})" reads "Attack (+5)", on its card too). A number or
  a formula can carry Value names (Not stunned, Physical, Mental): the
  number becomes a dropdown and the formula shows the name, while formulas
  still read 0, 1, 2. A caption or label that names a GM-only field never
  reaches players.
- Inline rows and counters: a section set to Inline rows draws each field on
  one line, label and value on the left and its Roll on the right. A number
  or skill ticked Counter gets − and + either side of its box. A section
  holding resources or counters can carry Reset all in its header, in your
  own words (Long rest): one press fills its resources and sets its counters
  back to their start. Players reset their own; your own reset comes back
  whole with one ↶.
- Show if: a section, or one placement (its "if" button), shows only while a
  formula is true (Stun > 0, Station == 2): a stunned banner, one station's
  actions. It is worked out on the character's own values, and the Layout
  preview draws a hidden part dimmed. It only hides: the values still reach
  the player, so a secret stays GM only.
- Handbook on the sheet: a section can carry a handbook chip in its header,
  and a Handbook link placement is a button; both open a handbook page, over
  the map beside the sheet for you and in the reader for a player. Players
  only ever get chips and links to pages they can read.
- Rich table: tick Rich table on an Item list field to show the inventory as
  a table of the columns you pick (category, cost, damage, area), with an
  expandable notes row, category chips and a totals footer.

Character sheets: the look
- The Sheet look box at the top of the Layout tab gives the sheet a look of
  its own over the campaign's default page look: a font, text and panel
  colours, and a background picture from the Image Library with a Dim slider
  (Clear goes back to the default). Players see the same look.
- Its shape: Headline or Accordion section titles (a line under each, a
  chevron on those that fold), Sticky titles (a section's title stays at the
  top while you scroll through it), Filled or Angular tabs, one Accent
  colour (Theme accent puts the gold back), Capital labels, and the
  character's portrait and name leading the header block.
- A Palette recolours the whole sheet at once (text, muted labels, panel,
  cards, input fields, edges, a primary colour, danger, good and warning)
  from a preset, Graphite (dark) or Parchment (light), or your own swatches;
  it looks the same under the light theme, and Clear palette puts the sheet
  back. Inter joins the font list.
- Finer options: Monospaced numbers, the band as an Inline band or Band
  chips, Arrows inside every number box, Boxed results, Item cards, and
  Effect cards (each status effect a card with a pill per change: +2 ST). On
  the Fields tab a formula can show as a Badge, each value name in its own
  colour (Good, Warning, Danger, Accent, Primary), and a resource can have
  its own colour; on the Rolls tab a roll takes a tone (filled, red, grey,
  outline) and an icon.
- Icons: every icon box (a tab, a section, a resource, an effect, an item, a
  roll) takes an emoji or one of 161 bundled icons from the picker beside it
  (Icons and Emoji tabs, with a search that knows words like spell, armour
  or rest), drawn in the sheet's own colours; Inter and the Font Awesome
  Free icons are credited in the About box. None of these options changes a
  sheet until you pick it.

Character sheets: rolls, effects and the token
- Situational modifier: shift-click (or Alt-click) a roll button on a sheet,
  or a field's die, to add a bonus or penalty first (quick chips, a stepper
  and a preview of the formula); a plus always helps, raising the total, or
  the target of a roll-under check. For a lone die (a d20, a d%) it also
  offers Advantage and Disadvantage: roll twice, keep the better or the
  worse. A plain click still rolls at once.
- Status effects: the System editor's Effects tab is a library of effects (a
  name, an icon, Buff or Debuff, a duration note, and changes that add to a
  number, a skill, a formula or a resource's max, or switch a toggle on),
  each Visible or GM only. A Status effects field on the sheet carries a
  character's: + Add effect… from the library, or New… made on the spot;
  untick one to suspend it, × to end it. Players change their own when the
  field is Player may edit.
- An effect reaches every formula, roll, hover card and header figure built
  on its field. The sheet shows the result beside the value you typed (→ 12,
  or ▲ / ▼ on a worked-out value) with each source in the tooltip
  (10 base · Rage +2); ST.base reads a value before effects, for point
  costs. When an effect that raised a max ends, a current value above the
  new max comes down to it.
- Facing dial: a Layout placement that shows the character's token facing as
  a hex (a square on a square grid); click a wedge or use the arrow keys to
  turn the token, and turning the token turns the dial. Click the outer ring
  to mark where a threat comes from (the first is active, later ones queue);
  Turn to face turns toward it. Formulas read Facing, Arc (0 front, 1 side,
  2 rear), Arc.front / .side / .rear and Threats, so a Dodge can vanish from
  behind: if(Arc.rear, 0, Dodge). It needs Token facing on (Settings ▸ VTT
  features).
- Stance: a Stance (posture & elevation) placement shows and sets the
  token's posture and elevation, the same values as its chip and right-click
  menu; formulas read Posture (0 standing to 6 lying face up) and Elevation
  (in yards). Players turn, mark and set their own token, never while
  paused.

Character sheets: pop-out, download and print
- The sheet panel resizes from its bottom-right corner (double-click the
  corner for the default size), remembered with its position. For you, ⧉ in
  its head pops the sheet out into its own window (drag it to a second
  monitor), a read-only copy that follows every change; Dock back in the app
  returns it.
- Download (the arrow in the sheet's head) saves the open sheet as a
  Character file (.wpchar.json: every value, the numbers worked out, its
  picture) or a Readable page (.md: every tab and section as text, which
  imports back as a handbook page). A player takes only their own sheet,
  exactly as they see it; you can take any character's, NPCs too, as its
  player would see it or, with Include GM-only fields ticked, your own full
  copy.
- Print or save as PDF… on the same menu prints the sheet exactly as it
  shows (its look, palette and background picture), then every tab in turn
  with every section open and notes in full; pick a PDF printer to keep a
  file. A print dialog no longer drops the connection: while one is open the
  other side of the table waits (up to three minutes), for a printed sheet
  and for Save PDF on a planner or page alike.

Character sheets: the HUD
- The HUD: a second, compact layout of the same sheet in a window of its
  own. The Sheet | HUD switch on the Layout tab lays it out with its own
  title, tabs, sections, band and ledger (a field can sit on both), from
  scratch or with "Copy the sheet's sections"; "Remove the HUD" takes it
  away, and the Sheet look dresses both. A change in one shows in the other
  at once.
- Opening it: HUD in the sheet's title bar, HUD… on a token's right-click
  menu, the party strip, Open HUD in the token's Properties, the Characters
  tab, or a HUD button you place on the sheet (which can open it at one of
  its tabs). One window per character, several at once, each dragged and
  resized from its corner (remembered); Esc closes. Players open their own
  character's, never with GM-only fields or rolls. The map's toolbars now
  sit under the sheet, a HUD and a handbook page instead of drawing over
  them.
- Roll history: a drawer at the foot of the HUD lists the rolls made as that
  character this session, newest first, a roll with a target in green or
  red. Clear hides them for you; it keeps 10, 25, 50 or 100.
- The bell beside it keeps the character's notes this session: an apply
  action's result, an effect applied, suspended, resumed, ended or run out,
  a start-of-turn reminder and the turn notes (your turn, a move counted or
  stopped, an action spent). Pin a note to keep it (pins stay on this
  computer and reach no one), remove one, or Clear the unpinned; it keeps
  12, 25 or 50.
- Three new placements for a sheet or a HUD. Handbook search lists the pages
  the viewer can read and searches them (headings as you type, the text on
  Enter), opening a result right there, with Open in reader for the full
  page; a section's handbook chip and a Handbook link then open their page
  in the character's HUD. Rules text shows a block of your own words as
  written. Custom roller is a formula box, a name and Roll: anyone who may
  roll as the character types any roll (3d6 <= DX + 2) and rolls it as them.

Character sheets: actions and consequences
- Apply actions: set a roll's Kind to Apply for a button that moves
  resources or numbers instead of rolling (Apply costs, Apply wounds): up to
  four changes, each Subtract, Add or Set to by an amount worked out for the
  character (FPCost, max(0, Incoming - DR)), all or nothing and never past a
  field's min and max. Its player and you may press it, and your machine
  works the amount out. Its card reaches the whole table only when every
  value it moves is on the hover card, else its player and you; Revert takes
  your press back whole.
- A roll's consequences: "+ Then…" under a roll adds up to four changes that
  happen after it lands, Always, On success or On failure of its test (a hit
  leaves a pending hit, a failed save adds a mark); with Show if, a Damage
  roll can show only while a hit is pending. For a player's roll your
  machine rolls the button's own formula, modifier and advantage included,
  so a hit cannot be made up. A roll's Malf (17, or a weapon's own) makes a
  natural total at or past it a malfunction: the card says so, it counts as
  a failure, and its On malfunction changes happen.
- Each round and at a turn's start: an apply action set to Also each round
  runs on every character in a combat whenever the round changes
  (Turn Set to CombatRound, Parries Set to 0); one set to Also at its turn's
  start runs on its character when its turn begins (a bleed, a reaction
  back). Either runs by itself or waits in chat as a reminder with a Run
  button, for you or for its player, in any combat, turn-based or not.
  Formulas read CombatRound, the round of the combat on the map of the
  character's token (0 with no token or no combat).

Turn-based combat
- Turns: the Combat card on the System editor's Items tab gains a Turns box.
  Move per turn is a formula worked out for each character (BasicMove,
  Speed) in feet, yards, metres or grid cells, which each map's scale
  converts; Diagonals on squares is a straight line, every diagonal 1
  square, or alternating 1–2 (the ruler counts them your way too); A round
  is so many seconds; your own time units (a watch) by their length; and the
  actions a turn allows (Action 1, Bonus action 1, Reaction 1). A roll or an
  apply action can cost one of them.
- Turn-based combat is a new VTT feature (Settings ▸ VTT features), on by
  default and yours to control for the whole table. While a combat runs, the
  player whose token has the turn gets End turn on the turn strip, their
  sheet and their HUD: it moves play on exactly as your Next turn does, and
  only while the turn is still theirs.
- Moves: a player's token in the combat moves only on its own turn, and then
  no further than its Move per turn, counted from where each drag began in
  the map's whole cells: from the cell the token stands in to the cell it
  lands in, on squares as on hexes (your diagonal rule still decides what a
  diagonal costs), so a token standing off the grid is no longer told it
  moved 2.1 squares. A map with no grid keeps the exact distance, and a turn
  in place or a shuffle inside its own cell counts nothing and says nothing.
  They hear "Your turn: 6 squares (30 ft) to move." and what is left after
  each move. A token outside the
  combat is free, and your own moves are never limited.
- Actions per turn: while their token is in the combat, a player's press of
  a roll or apply action that costs an action spends one, on or off their
  turn (a reaction); their actions come back when their turn begins, they
  hear what is left, and a spent button greys. Your own presses are never
  limited.
- Walls stop player tokens, in or out of combat: a player's token cannot
  land in or cross a cell a sight-blocker occupies (a wall, a pillar, a
  closed door; an open door never counts); it goes back to where its drag
  began, with a note. The setting sits under Settings ▸ VTT features ▸ Fog
  of war.
- Every refusal has its own Refuse, Warn or Off: Walls stop player tokens,
  and Move limit, Out of turn and Actions per turn under Settings ▸ VTT
  features ▸ Turn-based combat. Refuse (the default) puts the token back or
  refuses the press with the reason; Warn lets it through with a note to the
  player and to you (in the character's HUD bell too); Off does not check.
- Timed effects: a status effect whose duration reads as a time (3 turns, 10
  seconds, 1 minute, next turn, or your own unit: 2 watches) runs out by
  itself, with a private line to its player and you. In a combat a round
  passes at the start of each of its character's turns; outside one the
  clock runs, and the time left carries between encounters. Its row shows
  what is left, with pause, reset and stop (Effect timers, under Settings ▸
  VTT features ▸ Turn-based combat, says who may use them). Any other note
  (until dawn) stays until someone ends it.
- Range penalties: the Combat card gains a Range box, your game's penalty
  for distance, by a table (up to 10: -4; past the last row its modifier
  holds; up to 40 rows) or by a formula reading Distance, in yards, feet,
  metres or grid cells. Every ruler shows the modifier ("Range -4"), and
  each target mark carries it at its lower corner beside its cover and
  light. A hex grid counts hexes, a square one follows your diagonal rule,
  and between two tokens their heights count while Token elevation is on.
- Rolls read the range: any formula can use RangeMod (your range modifier to
  the target the character's player has marked, your own mark for an NPC)
  and TargetDistance (how far that target is), so a roll of
  3d6 + Skill + RangeMod takes its range penalty by itself, and a label such
  as Shot ({±RangeMod}) shows it on the sheet as the target changes. Both
  read 0 with no target, a target that player cannot see (one you hid, or
  one out of their sight on a fogged map), no token or offline; your
  machine works a player's roll out and judges that for them. A roll that
  reads them goes to its roller and you alone while another player on that
  map cannot see the target, so its card never tells them how far an unseen
  creature is (the player is told why; your own rolls do the same).
- Weapon ranges need no setting of their own: Help shows how a weapons
  list's Half and MaxRange stats, an Attack roll's Needs and a Damage
  formula refuse a shot past its longest range, apply the range penalty to
  ranged weapons only, and halve damage past half range.
- A list can carry up to twelve stats (ten before), so a weapons list that
  already has many has room for Half and MaxRange.
- A status effect that is switched on shows as a small icon on its
  character's token (ringed green for a buff, red for a debuff), for
  everyone who sees the token; a GM-only effect only for the GM.
- Right-click a token > Effects... adds or ends an effect from the map,
  without opening the sheet; a token with no character sheet keeps effects
  of its own (the library's, or one made on the spot). A player gets it on
  their own character's token where the sheet lets them change effects.
- A token that is not standing is a smaller target: a ruler that ends on it
  shows "-2 ranged (posture: kneeling)" after the distance, and its posture
  chip's tooltip and hover card say ranged attacks against it are at -2
  (their roll). Shown only, never rolled; it follows the Token posture switch.
- A sheet's Stance control says what the token's posture does under it: its
  changes, "Ranged attacks against you are at -2 (their roll, not yours)"
  for a smaller target, and its notes.
- The System editor's Combat card names your own postures: start from the
  seven, then rename, reorder or add (up to 12), each with a tag for its
  chip, notes, a Smaller target tick and changes like an effect's, applied
  while a token takes it. Formulas read Posture as its place in the list.
- A status effect can switch itself on: "Applies itself when" takes a
  formula (HP <= 0 for Unconscious), and while it is true the effect is on,
  marked Automatic on the sheet, on the token and in its Effects menu. Only
  a visible effect reading what players can read applies itself.
- The combat roster has Roll all: your system's initiative roll for every
  ticked character without a number yet. In a running fight a player's own
  initiative roll from their sheet takes its place in the order, once.
- The Combat card's Initiative box settles ties: up to three steps, each a
  value (the higher first, such as DX) or a roll made only for those still
  tied (1d6, d20), so a 3d6 game can order by Basic Speed and roll only for
  ties, and a d20 game roll and roll again.
- Hold a turn: on the turn strip, Hold lets the one on turn wait; Act later
  that round puts them right after whoever is acting, and they keep that
  place. A hold not used by the round's end is lost.
- Initiative rolled again every round, and side initiative: in the Combat
  card's Initiative box, tick Rolled again every round, or give a Sides roll
  (1d6) and each round the players' characters and everyone else roll it,
  the higher side first, each side in its own order.
- A turned shape or an image can block sight: flag it Blocks sight and it
  blocks the cells its outline covers, turned as it is drawn (never by its
  pixels). It gives cover as any wall does, and a turned door opens as any
  door.
- A pen line can be a wall: tick Blocks sight on it and it blocks as the
  line itself, a thin wall between cells. Sight, light, cover and players'
  moves stop at it; set to Door it opens and closes like any door.
- Height and cover: a piece's Height (its Properties) and your system's
  Height and cover rule (the Combat card): with token elevation on, from
  above a see-over piece gives no cover, from below it hides the target.
- The Combat card's Height box: a height modifier by how much higher the
  roller stands than the target, as a table or a formula; rolls read
  HeightMod and HeightDiff, and the ruler and target marks show it.
- Heights follow your Imperial or Metric choice: a token's elevation, a
  piece's height, the ruler's 3D line and a blast's labels, in yards or
  metres.
- Ground height: a shape, an image or a filled region can be a hill, a
  ledge or a pit. Give it a Ground height in its Properties and a token
  standing on it is that much higher, on top of its own elevation; where
  such pieces overlap, the highest counts. Cover by height, the ruler's 3D
  line, blasts and your rolls' HeightDiff read the two together.
- The Calendar (Settings ▸ VTT features; on by default): the
  campaign's date and time in the header. Its chip opens the calendar: the
  month as a grid by the week, steps to move time on (a minute to a week, or
  any amount, in your system's own units too), a day and time to set, and
  whether players see the date. Each round of a combat moves it on by the
  round's length, every move you make goes in the session log, and a player
  may hide it for themselves.
- Time rules: with the Calendar on, a roll or an apply action can also run
  by the clock, every so many minutes, hours, days or your own units,
  during rest only or any time (a daily HT roll that heals, FP back every
  10 minutes of rest). You move time on as Active or Rest; the calendar
  lists what is due, players' characters ticked, and Apply makes the rolls
  and sends each player a summary. In Settings, time rules can run at once
  or be off, and you can keep players from filling their own pools.
- With the Calendar on, a timed effect outside a fight runs by the
  campaign's clock instead of real time: each move of the clock on takes
  that much from every timed effect (a fight's turns count as before).
- Notes on dates: pick a day in the calendar and write a note on it, for you
  alone or for your players too; days with notes are marked, the next five
  are listed as Coming up, and you are reminded when the clock reaches one.
- The System editor's Calendar tab: the calendar your game keeps, its
  months and the festival weeks or holidays outside the month count, its
  week, the day's hours, how its years are numbered (counting down, if yours
  do) and leap years; start from the real-world year or a plain count of
  days.
- Difficult terrain: tick it in the fill menu, with its cost (x2 by default,
  up to x10), and the cells you paint are ground that costs that many times
  as much to move into; a rectangle, hexagon, circle or diamond takes the
  same mark in its Properties. On a square or hex grid a turn's Move per
  turn counts each difficult cell a move steps into at its cost (the note
  says what the move cost), and a ruler crossing difficult terrain shows the
  move's cost under its length.
- A target mark's cover, light and range now follow a token at once when
  you drop it on your own screen, not at the next redraw.

Items
- The System editor has an Items tab: your campaign's own weapons, gear and
  explosives. Each item carries a name, a category, an optional blast area
  (a radius in feet), a damage roll (dice allowed), a cost, an icon and a
  Visible/GM-only flag; a GM-only item stays out of players' lists, and
  players' sheets never carry item formulas.
- Characters carry items through a new Item list field on the sheet: an
  inventory with quantity, remove, and a + Add item… picker of the
  campaign's items. Players manage their own; your machine checks every
  change.
- Throw from the sheet: an item with a blast area gets a Throw button —
  press it, click the map, and the blast lands attributed to the character
  and shared with everyone on that map. Per campaign you choose the
  automation (full auto rolls and applies the damage, or roll-to-chat, or
  measure only), who rolls (the owner or the GM), and which resource damage
  subtracts from; a full-auto throw can be undone from the blast menu.
- The toolbar blast is now a GM quick-tool for an ad-hoc radius; the old
  fixed explosive presets are gone in favour of your campaign's own items.
- The System editor's Lists tab shapes each Item list field: the categories
  its picker offers, the same item more than once (a skill with two
  specialties), no quantity (skills and powers), a level per row with
  optional names (Broken, Accented, Fluent) and a switch per row (Readied,
  Equipped, Active, and whether new rows start on). Each row then shows its
  level and its switch, and its notes button holds the row's own note;
  players change these on their own characters. In Layout, a list can show
  only the rows switched on (readied weapons).
- Bound and cursed items: on an item in the Items tab, When a player removes
  it can make it Bound (it stays on their sheet with your message: "The ring
  won't come off") or Curse on contact (it leaves their sheet but stays on
  the character, out of their sight, until you remove it). When a player
  switches it off does the same for a list's switch. Players never see the
  setting, a mis-click in the first ten seconds can still be taken back, and
  you alone are told (a toast, and Items in the session log) when a player
  picks up, tries to remove or drops one.
- Stats and a price: a list can carry up to ten stats, numbers each of its
  items holds (Acc, Wt, Cost…) with a key, a label, a default and optional
  value names; the Items tab gives each item a box for every stat of its
  lists, and a stat set to On the row shows as a chip beside the row's name.
  One stat can be the list's price: a row records what it cost (Paid) when
  it is added, so a later price change never rewrites a purchase, and you
  can correct it in the row's Paid box.
- A copy's own values: ✎ on a row opens that copy's form (name, icon,
  category, notes, stats, blast, damage and cost, and its own locks). An
  empty box follows the library; a value you type is the copy's own (a gold
  dot marks it), ↺ takes one back and Follow the library takes them all.
  Item stats on players' sheets, at the top of the Lists tab, can let
  players change the stats of their own copies; a stat you set holds. Saving
  items that characters carry tells you how far each change reaches
  ("Blaster: affects 3 characters; 1 has its own Dmg."), and the session log
  keeps each line under Items.
- Custom rows: tick Custom rows on a list and players add items of their own
  with + Custom… in its picker: a New item row, its form open for a name, an
  icon, a category, notes, stats and a key. You can add one to any list
  shaped in the Lists tab and give it a blast, damage, locks or GM only; a
  player edits only the rows they made. An item deleted from the library
  stays on every character carrying it (not in library, on your sheet), and
  Make custom turns that copy into the character's own.
- Items that change the character: an item or a custom row can carry up to
  twelve changes (+ Change), as a status effect does: a number, skill or
  formula up or down, a resource's max, a toggle switched on. They count
  once per row, optionally per level (a trait at level 3, three times) and
  only while switched on (armour while Equipped); every row shows them as
  chips, and the value's breakdown names the item. A cursed item a player
  dropped keeps working on them, shown only as "an unseen effect".

Item lists in formulas
- Columns: a list can carry up to six formulas worked out for each row (no
  dice), reading the row as Row.lvl, Row.qty, Row.on, Row.paid, Row.<stat>
  or another column, and anything else on the character: a skill's cost from
  its level, a weapon's to-hit. Each has a label, a unit, value names,
  Hidden (worked out, not shown) and Total, summed under the list.
- Any formula can read a list: its totals (Skills.count, Weapons.paid,
  Weapons.Wt, a stat times the quantity, or a column summed), the same over
  the rows switched on (Weapons.on.Wt), or one row by its item's key
  (Skills.Karate.lvl, Weapons.Blaster.Acc), which also reads an item or
  library entry the character does not carry. A key no row has reads as not
  carried, a kept curse counts in no total, and whatever reads a GM-only
  value shows players "GM only".
- Choice stats: a stat can be a choice of named options, written
  Strength = STRmod, Dexterity = DEXmod (an option can read a list too:
  Guns = Skills.Guns.Target). An item, a copy or a custom row picks one, the
  sheet shows its label, and formulas read its value (Row.Attr,
  Skills.Karate.Attr). An option that reads a GM-only value never reaches
  players.
- Row rolls: up to four rolls per list (a label and a formula, dice allowed)
  put a button on every row, rolled with that row's names: a weapon's Attack
  as d20 + Row.Hit, a skill's Check as 3d6 <= Row.Skill. Shift-click adds a
  modifier; a roll on a GM-only item, or through a GM-only value, stays
  private.
- Row actions and counters: a list's roll can be an apply action too, its
  amounts read from the row (Apply costs on each power: FP − Row.FPCost). A
  list can keep up to four Counters on each row, such as a weapon's Charges
  and Hits, shown as chips with − and + ("Charges 12/20") and read as
  Row.Charges. A list's roll can move them (Fire: Charges − 1, On success
  Hits + 1) and can need something first (Row.Charges >= 1, "Out of
  charges"): its button greys with that text, and your machine refuses the
  roll.

The library
- Library… on the System editor's Items tab opens the campaign's library,
  for collections too big for one tab: thousands of weapons, spells or
  skills. It holds packs (each Players can see it or GM only) of entries: an
  item plus a description, tags, a reference (a book and page) and GM notes
  only you read. Search matches every word you type, and a pack of thousands
  opens and scrolls at once. The library is kept beside the campaign's save
  and goes with it into backups and exports.
- Several entries at once: Ctrl-click, Shift-click or Ctrl+A chooses them to
  move or copy to another pack, set their category, add or replace their
  tags, set who sees them, duplicate or delete them. An entry's form says
  how many characters carry it, and saving a change tells you how far it
  reaches, as a System save does for items.
- Import… reads a .wppack.json file and shows what it would do before
  anything changes (as a new pack, or merged into a pack by id or by key),
  counting what would be added, updated, unchanged, skipped or not valid,
  and naming any stats it would leave out (set up the lists, or import the
  system, first). Export… saves a pack as the GM's copy (everything) or a
  players' copy (no GM-only entries, GM notes or formulas).
- From the library… under an item list's + Add item… opens a picker over the
  sheet: a search, pack and category chips, and a preview with the entry's
  stats and your notes; Enter adds, Shift+Enter adds and stays open,
  Ctrl-click picks several. Each row keeps a copy of its entry, kept current
  as you edit the library, so the sheet still draws it if the entry is
  deleted.
- Players get the same picker on the lists they may change, over the packs
  you let them see: entries arrive a page at a time (the search covers what
  has come), an entry shows in full when highlighted, and your machine
  judges every pick. GM-only entries, GM notes and formulas never leave your
  machine.
- Your campaign's own items move into the library's Items pack the next time
  the campaign opens on your machine: a safety copy is taken first,
  characters keep carrying them and every formula reads the same. Items you
  make later in the Items tab move on the next open (the tutorial's Firepot
  stays).

ShadowBase sheets
- On a system built on the ShadowBase website's own keys, Character from
  this sheet (and Copy the sheet's values into the character, to copy again)
  brings the whole dossier across: the player, species, homeworld, levels
  bought, points, credits, alignment and the narrative, and its rows, from
  skills, advantages and powers to gear, worn armour with its DR per
  location, weapons, vehicles, cybernetics, hit locations and status
  effects. Each row is matched by name to an item or library entry and kept
  linked (its differences held as that copy's own values), or else made a
  custom row; the toast says what did not come across. Copy from a fresh
  export: the website mends older sheets when it opens them.
- Attaching a sheet also turns its token to the sheet's facing and marks its
  threats while it is engaged (with token facing on), and a skill level the
  website exports as text now comes across.
- Players update their own characters: Import on their sheet sends their
  ShadowBase file to you as proposed changes, and nothing changes until you
  review it. A gold Review (N) button appears on the character's sheet: each
  change has a tick (a value you set yourself and a row the file no longer
  has start unticked), Apply takes the ticked ones, Keep as it is takes
  none, and the player is told which. The Lists tab's Rules box can let the
  levels, switches and quantities of rows they already carry apply at once.

Handbook
- A third kind of item beside maps and planners: Handbook pages — rules,
  quick reference, a setting primer — written in the same block editor as a
  planner (plus sub-headings, plain tables and a horizontal line; no scene
  nodes, raw HTML or GM notes on a page), nested and re-ordered like
  planners, searched, exported (Export This Page / Export All Pages) and
  covered by the planner's undo.
- Layout: pictures, callouts, flares, tables, diagrams and flowcharts on a
  page take a width, float left or right with the text wrapping around them,
  a pixel nudge, or the full width of a multi-column section; a section
  heading splits what follows into 1–3 columns. In the preview, drag a
  picture to nudge it and its corner to resize it (one undo step per drag).
  A page flows like a web page: a player's narrower window, the print path
  and the HTML export reflow the same content.
- Import and export: a Markdown file (with its pictures picked alongside, or
  zipped with them; Google Docs' Markdown export works as is) becomes a
  planner or a handbook page — the import button in the planner toolbar
  appends to the open document, New planner (or page) from a Markdown file
  on the Planners and Handbook headers' right-click menu makes a new one,
  and the header Import button takes .md files and zips too. A preview lists
  every block (flip a type, untick a block) and nothing is written until you
  press Import: one undo step, Cancel leaves nothing behind. Save Markdown
  on the Export menu writes any planner or page back out (a zip when it has
  pictures), and Help ▸ Handbook ▸ Import and export holds the format with a
  template to download.
- Players at your table receive the pages you leave on "Players can read"
  (the default; the switch is in the planner toolbar and the page's
  right-click menu) and read them from a Handbook tree of their own — the
  only section of the left panel they see — in a panel over the play map, so
  the session keeps running underneath. Esc or × closes it. Your edits reach
  an open page as you type; turning a page GM only removes it from every
  player at once; only the hosted campaign's pages travel.
- Everything a player receives from a page is cleaned twice (on your machine
  before it is sent and on theirs before it is shown): text, never markup;
  links only to http(s); pictures only from the campaign; diagrams in a
  stricter mode. A deleted map, planner or page now leaves players' copies
  too.
- The Tutorial gains a House rules page, and the tour and Help cover the
  Handbook. Planner flowcharts and pages share one compiler.

Pictures per campaign
- The Image Library shows this campaign's pictures by default: every picture
  uploaded from its maps, planners and pages, used by them, or brought in.
  Shared (the tutorial art), Unfiled (pictures whose map, planner or page is
  gone) and All campaigns are a chip away. Import from another campaign…
  picks pictures from another campaign, Shared or Unfiled (Ctrl-click
  several) and brings them in: nothing is copied, this campaign remembers
  them. Handout pictures are picked from this campaign's too.
- Categories are per campaign now. Your existing categories moved into the
  campaign that owns or uses most of each one's pictures (the Tutorial's
  Default stays shared); a category can be made shared so every campaign
  sees it (marked ◇), and the tools on a chip rename, shelve, share or
  delete it. Names are unique across a campaign's and the shared list.
- Deleting a campaign turns its categories shared, so its pictures keep
  their tags under Unfiled. A picture's "used N times" now counts pages,
  planners, the cast and campaigns that brought it in. Campaign and
  Everything exports bundle a campaign's own pictures even when nothing
  references them yet.
- The ★ Campaign Cast shows in the Image Library under the campaign's own
  chip only, not under Shared, Unfiled or All campaigns. Clicking a cast
  face opens a preview (portrait, name and stat line) with Add to map
  instead of dropping a token at once, and Ctrl-click and Shift-click pick
  several to add together.

Sound
- Ambient loops and one-shot cues at the table. 🎵 Sound, under 🎬 Scene on
  the play map (GM only), opens a small panel that stays open while you run
  the scene: click a loop to crossfade to it, a cue to fire it (up to four
  overlap), Master for the table's volume, Mute mine for your own speakers.
  Every player hears what you play; the speaker in the Table pill gives each
  of them their own volume, a mute, and Off for me at this table.
- Library…: this campaign's sounds. Add MP3, OGG, M4A or WebM files (4 MB
  each; loops up to two minutes, cues cut after thirty seconds), name them,
  flip Loop / Cue, set a gain, preview, delete. Shared is the bundled set:
  seven ambient loops (rain, forest, dungeon, cavern, night crickets, wind,
  campfire) and twelve cues (door opens / closes, latch, coins, footsteps,
  page flip, blade drawn, creak, dice, sword clash, healing, teleport), all
  CC0 and credited in assets/sounds/NOTICES.txt, about 1.3 MB in all; a
  bundled sound is hidden per install rather than deleted (Show hidden
  brings it back). Import from another campaign… brings sounds in by
  reference, nothing copied. Files live in saves/images/audio/<campaign>/
  and travel to players over the connection one at a time, the playing loop
  first, so a player who joins mid-scene hears the loop once its file has
  crossed.
- Sound is a VTT feature: per campaign in Settings ▸ VTT features, a default
  for new campaigns, and the GM's campaign is the ceiling at a table (a
  player can turn it off for themselves, never on). The stream window is
  silent unless "Stream window plays the table's ambient sound" is ticked.
  In a browser tab one click on the page is needed before sound may play;
  the speaker pulses until then.

Music
- Music, separate from Sound: 🎶 Music, under 🎬 Scene on the play map,
  holds the campaign's songs (+ Add music… uploads MP3, OGG and other
  compressed audio files of up to 25 MB each; not WAV or FLAC) and named
  playlists built from them, with play and pause, previous and next, a
  seek bar, loop (off, one song or the whole list), shuffle and a speed
  from 0.5× to 2×.
- Remember on this map gives a map a song or a playlist: during a session it
  starts on each player's machine as they enter the map, and carries on
  without a restart onto a map with the same music (tick Auto-play map music
  while solo to hear it while you prepare alone). Take control of the
  table's music plays the same track for everyone until you release it; then
  each player hears their own map's music again.
- Songs travel to players over the connection. The 🎶 note in the Table pill
  gives each player their own music volume and mute, apart from Sound. Music
  is a VTT feature (Settings ▸ VTT features), and a player can switch it off
  for themselves.
- Music from another campaign: From another campaign… in the Music panel's
  Library lists your other campaigns that have music. Tick playlists (each
  comes with its songs) and single songs and bring them in. Nothing is
  copied: this campaign lists the other one's files, a song it already
  lists is not added twice, and a brought-in song says which campaign it
  is from. Taking a song out of the library never touches its file.
- A campaign file merged into a campaign already here brings its music too
  (its playlists and songs, and the maps that came with it still find their
  remembered music); before, a merge left the file's music behind. A song
  or a playlist the campaign already holds is updated by the file's, as a
  merged map is, so merging a newer copy never leaves two of a playlist.

Video
- Video is a new VTT feature (Settings ▸ VTT features, on by default): the
  🎬 Scene menu has a Video panel for the campaign's videos. Add videos...
  copies MP4 or WebM files of any size into your saves folder, with a
  progress line; a file this app cannot play, or one with no picture, is
  refused. Each video has a name you can change, its length, size and
  picture size, a button to watch it in the panel and one to delete it with
  its file. The panel moves by its head, resizes from its corner and goes
  full screen.
- Show a video to your players, live: while you host, a video on the
  panel's stage has Show to players, for everyone or the players you tick.
  They see and hear what plays in your panel (you play, pause and seek for
  all) in a panel of their own that they move, resize, mute and put full
  screen; closed, a chip in the header reopens it while it plays. It closes
  on their screens when the video ends or you stop it, and Loop keeps a
  mood clip running. Nothing is downloaded, a player who joins late is
  shown it too, and the panel names who is being sent it.
- An export carries the campaign's videos, whole: Export This Campaign and
  Export Everything bundle each video file with the pictures and sounds, and
  an import puts them back in place. An export has no size limit now: files
  are read from disk and written in parts, an archive past 4 GB or 65,535
  files is written as ZIP64, and it is refused rather than written wrong.
  When the videos and music pass 1 GB the export asks where to save it and
  writes straight to that file (it is never held in memory), then reads it
  back and checks every file in it before calling it exported.
  An import opens the file without reading it whole and checks every file
  against its checksum before copying it: a damaged one is counted as
  failed, never written.
- The local server answers a byte range: a long video or song loads in
  parts and seeks without being read whole, and a video or a sound is
  served under its own type.

Dice
- Roll at the table: type /roll 2d6 + 3 in Table Chat (or /r), or open the
  dice roller beside the message box: quick buttons for d4 to d100, a Roll
  button, and the up arrow for your last twenty rolls. The GM's machine
  makes every roll and everyone sees the same card: the formula, every die
  with what was kept or dropped, the total, or for a check the margin, and a
  mark on a lone d20 showing 20 or 1. The full syntax (4d6kh3, 2d6!, d20r1,
  10d6cs>=5, 4dF, d%, checks like d20 + 5 >= 16) is in Help ▸ Dice.
- Private rolls: /gmroll (or /gr) rolls for the GM's eyes only; a player's
  /gmroll rolls to the GM; a whisper target picked in the chat panel makes
  the GM's /roll private to that player. Every table roll lands in the
  session log under Dice. Rolls work without a session too, as a local card.
- The bundled Dice cue plays on each roll for those who want it (the Sound
  box in the roller). Dice is a VTT feature: per campaign in Settings ▸ VTT
  features, your campaign the ceiling at a table; a player who turns it off
  for themselves loses the roller and the sound, not the cards.
- Table chat now clears when a session ends or a new one starts, so a new
  table never sees the last one's lines; the dev console keeps /roll local
  and gains /table <expr> to post a roll from there.
- Table Chat opens by itself when a roll lands while it is closed, so the
  card is seen, and closes again after a few seconds unless you open or
  touch the chat or the roller.
- Roll outcomes (the Combat card on the System editor's Items tab) can read
  3d6 roll-under criticals: a roll of exactly 3d6 against a target reads 3–4
  as a critical success (5 at a target of 15 or more, 6 at 16 or more), 17
  as a failure (critical at 15 or less), and 18, or failing by 10 or more,
  as a critical failure. The card, the session log and what a roll changes
  on success or failure all follow it; the default stays success or failure
  by the margin.
- Roll cards take a new shape: the roll's name as a bold title and a
  coloured stripe down the left edge (the plain colour, green for a
  success, red for a failure), in the colours of the system's sheet look
  where it has one; a private card is framed in violet. A roll ticked
  Damage in the System editor (a roll, or a list's roll) posts a damage
  card: the damage colour, a larger total and a "damage" tag; a thrown
  item's damage does too.

Visual effects
- A visual-effects panel on the play map (✨ Visual effects, under 🎬 Scene)
  fires short-lived effects the players on that map see: a flash, screen
  shake, a colour wash (darkness, red mist, blinding light) that holds until
  you clear it, a burst you place on the map (boom, magic, smoke, sparks),
  weather that runs until you stop it (rain, snow, embers, haze), and a text
  banner. Right-click a token for a pulse; the Blast tool gains a Boom.
- Ping: Alt+click on the play map (or the panel's Ping button, then the map)
  rings that cell for a moment, drawn over the fog, for everyone on the map;
  the Ping row's list sends pings to one player on it instead, and Alt+click
  follows the list until that player leaves the map. A player's screen draws
  at most eight at once, and Reduce motion keeps the ring still.
- Everything is drawn on your machine — no files, nothing saved. "Sound with
  it" fires a chosen cue alongside. Reduce motion (per machine) skips shake
  and shortens flashes, and a hard flash guard protects against strobing.
  Visual effects is a VTT feature (Settings ▸ VTT features); a player can
  switch it off for themselves and still see the map.

Fog of war
- Cover a map and reveal only what the party can see. The fog button on the
  play map (a fog bank, GM only) opens a panel: fog on or off for this map,
  a reveal / hide brush, and Reveal all or Cover all at once; on a gridless
  map a ruleset and cell size give the brush its measure. Fog starts off on
  every map — turn it on where you want it. While a fog map is open you
  always see the see-through fog overlay, across every tool and while
  dragging items, not only in fog mode.
- Sight: a default sight range, and a "sight from" picker that reads each
  character's range from a field on their sheet, falling back to the
  default. Sight counts in yards, feet, metres or grid cells, and each map's
  scale converts it (60 ft on 5 ft squares is 12 squares). Preview any
  player's view from the panel to see the map exactly as they will.
- Players see an opaque fog over everything their own characters cannot see,
  and never receive what is hidden: your machine works out each player's
  vision and drops the creatures they cannot see from what it sends, so
  nothing behind the fog reaches their machine. Fog of war is a VTT feature
  (Settings ▸ VTT features, with a default for new campaigns), on by default
  and never a player's to enable.
- Sight-blocking: flag a fill or a shape (rectangle, hexagon, circle/pillar
  or diamond) as "Blocks sight" in its Properties and it becomes a wall — a
  token's fog vision, and each player's view, stops at it (fog must be on
  for the map).
- Doors: set a sight-blocker's type to Door — closed it blocks, open it lets
  sight through. You toggle a door from its Properties or by clicking it in
  fog mode; a player can open or close one their token stands next to
  (unless you lock it). A height dimension comes next.
- Vision — all around or a facing cone: a map's fog menu has a Vision
  setting, all-around (a token sees every direction) or a facing cone whose
  width you choose and which turns with the token. It's set per map and not
  tied to the grid type — a square map can use a cone, a hex map can see all
  around — with a "use for new maps" campaign default that never changes
  maps you already made.
- Lighting, a VTT feature on by default: a fogged map is lit until you place
  a light source, so every token sees what is in its line of sight (walls
  and closed doors still stop it), and a token's sight becomes how far it
  sees in the dark. The fog menu sets a map's light: Auto, Bright, Dim
  (beyond a token's sight in the dark the map shows darkened) or Dark (a
  token sees only as far as its sight). Place a light source from the
  effects panel (Light source, then click the map) and give it a bright and
  a dim radius in its Properties; a token can carry a light too. Light
  spreads bright to the first radius and dim to the second, walls and closed
  doors stop it, and players see a light, never its marker. A torch carried
  round a corner lights what a player sees without telling them who holds
  it. With Lighting off, a token sees only as far as its sight, as before.
- Light by your system's rules: the Combat card (the System editor's Items
  tab) has a Light box for what your game calls a dim and a dark place and
  for its light presets (a torch, a lantern), each with its radii in yards,
  feet, metres or grid cells. A light's Properties offer the presets. Name
  the levels, wording your penalty into the name, and the ruler between two
  tokens shows the light the far one stands in, and a target mark carries
  it at its corner with the name under the target: on the GM's screen for
  every targeter with a token on the map, on a player's for their own
  mark. The starter systems come with a few lights.
- A player's own light: a player switches the light of their own token off
  or on, or picks one of the lights you ticked Players may pick, from the
  token's right-click menu, on any map. Your machine copies the light from
  your own list, so no number is taken from a player, and you are told.
  Lock a token's light in its Properties to keep it yours alone. Your Undo
  leaves the light of a player's token as it is now, your own changes to
  it included. A token's or a light source's right-click menu switches its
  light for you as well.
- The Tutorial's Dark Cellar shows light at work: Bren carries a torch from
  the Tutorial's own light presets, a brazier burns in the far corner and is
  seen from across the room, and the Cellar Lurker stands in the dark
  between them, out of his sight. A Tutorial you already have gains them
  the next time the tour starts or you open it from Help: the torch and the
  light presets where it has none of its own, the brazier where its cellar
  is still Dark and holds no light source of yours.
- The Tutorial's cellar shows senses too: its system's Hearing lets Bren's
  player hear the Cellar Lurker in the dark as a nameless mark, and a patch
  of smoke near the brazier hides part of its glow from Bren. A Tutorial you
  already have gains them the next time the tour starts or you open it from
  Help: the Hearing where its system has no senses of its own, the smoke
  where its cellar holds none.
- Cover from the map: the walls, pillars, closed doors and filled cells that
  block sight also give cover, and a piece's Cover setting in its
  Properties can make it cover you can see over (a crate or a low wall:
  half or three-quarters, never total) or no cover at all. Turn on Cover
  from blockers on the Combat card (the System editor's Items tab) and drag
  the ruler between two character tokens — it reports the cover between
  them (half / three-quarters / total, or a simple cover/none for systems
  that grade it once), on square and hex. A thrown blast's labels show each
  token's cover from the blast, and a small tag at the corner of each target
  mark shows the cover between the targeting token and its target. Cover
  never changes a roll: Waypoint estimates it and you apply it. The one
  exception is a full-auto blast, where the Combat card sets what each grade
  of cover does to its damage (full, half or none; full until you change
  it). A blast thrown into a wall or a closed door goes off in front of it,
  on the thrower's side, and a large token is as exposed as its most exposed
  cell.
- Play areas: mark an image or shape as a play area (Play area in its
  Properties, or the ▣ button on the selection toolbar) and fog covers only
  the cells under marked items, leaving scenes and map art around them lit.
  When no play area is marked, in the fog menu, chooses for the campaign
  between fogging the whole map, as before, and no fog until you mark one.
  Hidden items never fog. A large play area's outline is traced once while
  the view stays still, not again on every frame.
- Fog on · all maps, in the fog menu, turns fog on for every map of the
  campaign at once, and New maps in this campaign start with fog on covers
  the maps you add later.
- The coordinate rulers along the play map's top and left edges now draw
  over the fog, so you and your players can read them on a fogged map; they
  show board coordinates only, nothing under the fog.
- Senses: the System editor's Combat card holds your game's senses besides
  the eyes (darkvision, blindsight, a sense of the Force). Each sees every
  cell within its range, and the creatures there reach its player as the
  eyes would show them. A range comes from a field of the character (one
  its player can read) or is a number every character has; you pick what
  it counts in, whether walls stop it, whether it sees all round and
  whether it sees the dark as dim, and each sense says in a sentence what
  it does. Sight stays the eyes' own range in the dark; a token sees by the
  longer of the two. The fog menu names the system's senses. A character
  token's Properties show each sense as that token has it on its map, and
  can give the token a range of its own (a creature's sense, a plain
  token's; 0 switches the sense off for that token); only that token's
  player receives it. Properties also hold a Blind tick: a blind token's
  eyes see only its own cell, whatever the light, while a sense that does
  not use the eyes still works. A token is blind too when its character's
  sheet says so (the system's blind switch, a Blinded effect). In the System
  editor each sense can be ticked "Uses the eyes" (off while blind) or
  switched off by a field of its own (Deafened), "Blind when" names the
  field that makes a character blind, and "Sees without eyes" starts a
  sense that works blind. A blind player's screen says "Blind" above their
  token with the senses that still work, and a token's right-click menu
  names its senses in one line.
- Marks: a sense can mark what it finds instead of showing it. A creature a
  player's mark sense finds but their eyes do not appears on their fog as a
  nameless mark in its cell, told apart by its shape (three arcs for a
  sound, a zigzag for a tremor, a ring round a dot for a presence, a
  teardrop for heat), with its word (Heard, Felt, Sensed, Heat) while the
  pointer is on it and a count at the bottom left of the board. Nothing of
  the creature reaches the player, only the cell. The marks swell slowly
  and hold still under reduced motion. A token's Properties can keep it
  from any mark sense ("Never shown as a mark by"), and the GM's preview
  of a player shows that player's marks. In the System editor's Senses box
  a row can show everything in range or a nameless mark, and a mark row
  picks its mark; Hears (off while your Deafened toggle is ticked), Feels
  through the ground, Senses what is near and A creature's sense (a range
  of 0 until a token is given one) start one.
- Marks in a fight (the fog menu, shown when the system has a mark sense):
  As they move, the default, lets marks follow every move. On your own
  turn holds each player's marks on a map while a fight runs there, where
  they were heard, until a turn of one of their tokens starts, and follows
  their own drops on that turn. A player with no token in the fight gets
  theirs at each new round, and everyone's are taken when the fight
  starts. A mark still goes at once when its creature comes into view, is
  hidden or leaves, and the count says "as heard on your turn". It stops a
  lone player matching a moving mark to a turn in the order; it does not
  hide what players tell each other.
- Snap decides where a dropped token lands, on every grid. With Snap on it
  lands in a cell: centred in it on a hex map, on the lattice on a square
  one, and on a dot of the background on a map with no grid. With Snap off
  it lands exactly where you drop it, hex maps included (before, hex maps
  always seated character tokens). Arrow keys follow it too: with Snap on a
  token hops one cell at a time, off it moves by pixels. Your players' drops
  follow your Snap. A token you fitted with Fit to Grid stays in its cell.
- A player's drop, turn, stance, drawing, erase or facing marks is no longer
  undone when it crosses something your machine sends at the same moment
  (your save, another player's change): every map you send says which of
  that player's actions it already holds, and their app keeps the ones it
  does not, on their screen and in what it sends next. It needs both of you
  on this version; with an older GM's Waypoint a player's app works as before.
- A Sight taken from a character field its players cannot read (GM-only, or
  worked out from one) is no longer used: their tokens see by the default
  range, on your screen and theirs alike (before, your machine judged by the
  hidden number while their screen drew the default). The fog menu says so.
- Players remember what they have seen (the fog menu, off until you tick
  it): each player's own screen keeps the ground they have seen this
  session, dimmed further than a dim cell. Remembered ground shows every
  piece on it that is not a character token, as it is now, while creatures
  there stay hidden. Ground seen only through a sense that passes walls is
  not kept; hiding a cell by hand takes it out of memory, and Cover all or
  Reveal all clears everyone's memory of the map.
- Null areas: tick "Senses fail here" in a shape's or a picture's
  Properties, and a token whose cell it covers loses those senses (a place
  where the Force is silent, a room that muffles sound). It works hidden:
  a hidden area never reaches players, not even its outline. The player
  whose token stands in one is told which of its senses fail there, and
  the caption over their token says so ("Hearing fails here").
- Smoke: tick Smoke in a rectangle's, hexagon's, circle's or diamond's
  Properties and it hides what is in it and past it from the eyes, and
  from a sense the walls stop unless the System editor ticks that sense
  "Sees through smoke" (a sense that passes walls always does). A token in
  smoke still sees its own cell. Tokens walk through it, it gives no cover,
  light passes, and a hidden piece hides nothing.
- The two starter systems now carry their game's senses: the d20
  Darkvision, Truesight, Blindsight and a Tremorsense mark, the 3d6
  Infravision and a Vibration Sense mark, with Blinded and Deafened
  toggles and an effect for each. Every range starts at 0 and only the GM
  sets them, in a character's Senses section, so a new campaign plays as
  before until a range is entered. A campaign begun from a starter earlier
  keeps the system it has.
- Height in the fog: with your system's Height clears low cover rule, a
  creature behind a crate or a low wall is hidden from a player whose
  tokens all stand below it, while it stands no higher than the piece. The
  piece stops what a wall stops there; a sense that passes walls still
  marks the creature, and one token of theirs that sees it keeps it.
- True 3D sightline (the Combat card's Height box): a piece with a Height
  stops a line of sight or cover only where the straight line from the
  viewer's eye to the target runs at or below it, so a token sees a
  creature over a low wall, and the ruler, target marks and blasts read
  cover the same way. Standing height is how far above its elevation a
  token's eye sits; a sight-blocker with no Height is full height.

Drawing tools

- Fill bucket (GM): a new tool that colours grid cells. Click or drag cells

Security
- A player who joins your table receives only the campaign you are hosting:
  your other campaigns (their names, maps and planners) no longer reach
  them, and their campaign list offers the hosted one alone.
- A character is only ever looked up by its own id: a token in a campaign
  file, or a host, naming a character by one of JavaScript's built-in names
  (such as "__proto__" or "constructor") finds nothing, so a Full auto blast
  or a character update can no longer write into the app's own objects.
- Players you have hosted before are recognised by a private table key their
  copy of Waypoint receives when you first let them in, not by the name they
  give; someone using a known name without it waits for you to let them in,
  with a hint saying so. Exported campaigns never carry the keys.
- Your machine checks what players send more strictly: a player waiting to
  be let in receives nothing of the table; chat always shows its real
  sender; floods of moves, edits, chat and picture requests are limited; a
  pause stops a player's sheet edits, rolls and throws too; a request for a
  picture or a sound reaches only your picture and sound files, never the
  rest of your save; and a player opens only doors on the map they are on.
- What a table sends a player is cleaned before it is shown: text on the map
  keeps only safe formatting, pictures come only from the table, and profile
  pictures and names are checked before they are drawn, on the GM's screen
  too.
- The local server behind the app answers only Waypoint's own pages: another
  web page open in your browser, or another machine on your network, can no
  longer read or change your save, and an upload lands only among your
  pictures. Links in shared content open only as web links, in your browser,
  and an update without a valid checksum is refused.
- A campaign imported from someone else's file shows safely on your own
  screen: text is escaped or cleaned where it appears, colours must be
  colours and numbers numbers, diagrams lose their click actions, and no
  picture loads from outside the app. Import Replace cleans a file as a load
  does, and deleting an image category's files keeps the pictures another
  campaign still uses.

Under the hood
- This release changes Waypoint's core (the local server behind the app,
  which now stores the campaign library and carries the new security
  checks): updating from inside an older Waypoint walks you through the
  installer, and the library needs the new core.
- Text saved through the app's local server is decoded whole even where a
  character straddles two pieces of a large save, so an em dash, an accent
  or an emoji is never stored as "�".

- Each map and each planner now has its own undo and redo. Ctrl+Z takes back

  quietly roll one of them back.

Multiplayer

  you hide it or it moves out of their sight: a row read "Hidden" before,

  Update Combat takes a creature out once it has left.

  as your target, so a player who clicked to look at a token targeted it (and
  the GM was asked to start combat) without meaning to. Players now target by

Fixes

WAYPOINT 1.1.1 — What's New
============================

Fixes from the first round of table testing
- Eraser now rubs out only the part of a stroke you touch; the rest of the
  drawing stays (it splits into pieces around the gap).
- Hex grid: tokens seat exactly into cells everywhere on the map. The grid
  tile is now a whole 52 px wide — the old 51.96 px tile drifted a hair per
  column, which is why tokens looked slightly off far from the origin.
- Data map: Select/Move and Pan tools join Link Mode, the map never starts
  in Link Mode, Link Mode lights gold while active, and a Clear-the-board
  button (with confirmation) wipes every room and link.
- The properties panel no longer goes blank on older maps missing categories.
- Typing a node's name or a character's name no longer rebuilds the panel
  under your cursor — it used to jump to the top and drop focus, so the next
  keys hit the board (Delete removed the node, arrows nudged it). The panel
  now keeps your scroll position and caret through every refresh.
- Fixed a crash ("m.toast is not a function") when linking rooms.
- Camera starts at the true canvas center on maps without a saved home.
- Minimap no longer drifts while you drag across it.

Multiplayer crash recovery
- If Waypoint closes unexpectedly while you're hosting, relaunch it and press
  Host again: you get the SAME room code back (for 15 minutes), and players
  still reconnecting reattach on their own. Players now keep trying for about
  two minutes instead of thirty seconds. Ending a session on purpose retires
  the code as before.
- Tokens never straddle hex cells any more: every token in a multi-drag,
  arrow-key nudges (which now hop cell to cell on hex maps), pasted copies,
  spawned player tokens, and player drops arriving over the network are all
  seated into a cell — the GM's copy is the authority.
- Players can grab their token even when a text box, note card, or trigger
  zone is drawn over it.
- Portals work by dropping: a player who drops their token on a door, stairs,
  or any portal-linked item travels through it — no double-click needed. The
  GM can push a player through by dropping that player's token on a portal.
- Clicking away from a selection (anywhere but the board, the Properties
  panel, menus, or dialogs) clears it, so the floating toolbar never lingers.
- A connection dot on the 🌐 button shows the table's real health at all
  times: green = live, amber = the other side has gone quiet or you're
  reconnecting, red = lost. Hover it for details. Both sides now exchange a
  heartbeat every few seconds, so a GM whose app died is noticed within about
  half a minute (players start reconnecting; the GM sees ⏳ on a silent player
  and drops them if the silence continues) instead of looking "connected".
- The installer refuses to close a running Waypoint silently, and asks first
  when run by hand — an update can no longer kill a live table.

Play Map
- Shapes and text are placed where you click — or drag to draw the exact box
  they should fill. Esc cancels.
- Text color: the color swatch (toolbar or Properties) now colors the text,
  with a custom color picker, and text boxes get their own tooltip.
- Lock proportions while resizing (toolbar button, Properties checkbox, or
  hold Shift while dragging).
- Player visibility is front and center: a Visible/Hidden box at the top of
  the Properties panel explains what players see, and the toolbar eye turns
  red when an item is hidden.
- Drawings with Snap on now ride the grid LINES: a fast swipe walks from
  vertex to vertex along cell edges instead of cutting across cells (square
  and hex grids).
- Fixed snapped drawings (straight lines especially) rendering ~6px below
  their true position — the stroke's SVG sat on a text baseline.
- Every color row in Properties (fill, pen, text, box background) ends in a
  custom color wheel; the current color is outlined.
- Text wraps inside its box (long words too), and multi-line text stacks
  properly instead of laying paragraphs side by side.
- Text boxes can be styled: font (the standard Windows families), text size,
  horizontal and vertical alignment, and a box background (tints, solids,
  shade/frost, or any custom color) — all in Properties.
- The selection toolbar's color swatch opens a palette that matches what you
  selected — inks for text and drawings, fills plus inks for shapes, and a
  custom picker on all of them. Click the swatch again (or anywhere else) to
  close it.
- Every tool has its own pointer: a pen for Draw, a crosshair-ruler for
  Measure, a crosshair-plus while placing a shape, a hand for Pan, a link
  arrow in the data map's Link Mode — and the eraser hides the pointer so
  only its reach circle shows. Items no longer flash the grab hand under
  a tool that isn't Move.
- Eraser: a circle around the pointer shows its reach (solid while pressed),
  its size is adjustable (click the eraser again), and it has two modes —
  Precise trims strokes exactly to the circle's edge (erase just what you
  pass over), Segments removes each point-to-point piece the circle touches.
- Hover tooltips can no longer be highlighted or left behind; any click,
  Esc, or moving off the item hides them.
- Click any placed measuring ruler to remove it.
- Center menu gains "Center on Character" — your own token as a player.
- Tokens follow rooms: drop a character token onto a shape linked to a room
  and the character moves into that room's list on the data map.
- The mouse wheel zooms on its own now (no Ctrl needed), 5% a tick toward
  the pointer; Shift + wheel scrolls the board.
- Hexagons resize like every other shape. A "New shape size" switch in the
  Shapes menu picks Free (click = default, drag = exact box) or Grid cell
  (every new shape arrives one cell in size and seats into the cell).
- Drawings' Properties now match the pen: the pen's color palette plus a
  custom color, and a pen-size slider.
- The lock button shows the item's state (closed padlock = locked), and the
  proportion lock has its own icon (dashed box + diagonal arrow, padlock when on).
- The hex grid is flat-top now: a flat side faces up, so "forward" on a token
  is a face of its cell. Cells are 60 wide × 52 tall (they were 52 × 60,
  point-up). Saved hex maps re-seat their tokens and hex shapes onto the new
  cells the first time they load.
- Tokens turn in cell-side steps while a grid is on: four facings on a
  square grid, six on a hex grid (one per face). Hold
  Shift while turning for free 15° steps. The Rotation slider and number in
  Properties land on the same facings. Turn a token from its own arrow:
  click it for the next facing (Shift-click goes the other way), drag it to
  aim. Properties → Turning picks whether the art turns with the arrow or
  only the arrow moves around an upright picture. With no grid they turn
  freely; applying a grid turns every token to its nearest facing. A small
  gold arrow marks each token's front and always points across a cell face
  at a neighbouring cell; pick which side of the art is the front under
  Properties → Front Side. Players can now turn the token assigned to
  them, and the whole table sees it turn live.
- The hover card over an item sits a little further from the pointer so the
  cursor never covers it.
- The Properties sidebar stays closed until you select something (a room on
  the data map, an item on the play map) and closes again when the selection
  clears. Open or close it by hand with the ▶ button and it stays that way
  until you select something else.
- The floating toolbar over a selection keeps one screen size at every zoom
  level (it used to scale with the board and jump while you zoomed), and it
  sits higher so it no longer covers the blue rotate dot.
- Token condition: right-click a token (or Properties → Condition) to mark it
  Alive, Incapacitated (red X over the token) or Dead (skull, art darkened).
  The whole table sees it.
- Size matching while resizing is unmistakable now: when your item pauses
  at a neighbour's width or height, that neighbour lights up gold, a bright
  bar runs along the matched edge of both items, and a badge names the axis
  and the neighbour ("↔ WIDTH 300 = Scene art"). Works with Snap off and with
  proportions locked — the other side follows the ratio. The blue guide lines that snaps
  draw were invisible because of a typo; they show again.
- Snap has a mode now: click the Snap button again for Snap To — Grid (cells
  only, the most predictable), Items (neighbours only, for tidy rows), or
  Both (the old behaviour: neighbours first, grid for open-space drops).
  Default is Grid. Remembered per computer; the menu also turns snapping off.
- Fit to Grid: the ⊞ button in the selection toolbar (and in Properties)
  sizes the selection to whole grid cells and seats it — tokens and hex
  shapes become exactly one hex cell; other items round to whole cells.
- The selection toolbar and the resize (gold) and rotate (blue) dots follow
  the item live while you drag, resize, rotate, or zoom, instead of catching
  up when you let go — and the dots stay the same size on screen at any zoom.
- Groups and multi-selections resize together: a single corner handle sits on
  the selection's corner; drag it and everything scales from the top-left
  (Shift keeps proportions).
- An image's frame is the picture: the box takes the image's real proportions
  when it loads, so the selection outline and handles hug the art with no
  invisible margins, and images keep their proportions when resized unless
  you unlock them.
- Duplicate any item — ⧉ in the selection toolbar, the right-click menu,
  a Duplicate button in Properties, or Ctrl+D. The copy carries every
  setting and everything attached (character sheet, stats, portal target,
  styling, layer, lock); groups copy as a new group; a copied player token
  is GM-controlled until you assign it. Hex tokens land one cell over.
- Elements panel: rename any element (✎ or double-click; also a Name field in
  Properties), rows are grouped by layer in stacking order — front at the top,
  back at the bottom — and ▲/▼ move an element a layer at a time.
- The T (text) button lights up while you're placing text, switches the board
  out of draw/erase/measure/pan first, and a selected text box's toolbar
  swatch shows an "A" over a color bar so it reads as text color.

Portals & triggers
- Warps land you on the right ROOM, not just the right map: a node that
  leads to a map arrives at the room there with the same id or name (or the
  Landing Room you pick in Properties), with the camera on it and the room
  selected. Players sent through a portal arrive — and their token spawns —
  on that room's play map item. (This is the "Belas's office goes to the
  prison block" fix: the office is a room inside that map.)
- Any play map item can be a portal on its own: Properties → Portal to Map.
  On a nested map the parent is offered first ("Back to …"), so a way home
  is one click — no helper node needed. Pick a marker (door, stairs, gate…).
- Trigger zones come in any shape — square, circle, diamond, hexagon — via
  Properties → Trigger Shape, and the Trigger button picks the shape that
  fits the grid: hex zones on hex maps, square zones on square grids.

Category library
- Categories can be saved once and reused on any map in the campaign: press
  ☆ on a category (or Save all to library), then on another map pick it from
  Category Library → Add, or Add all missing. Same names and colors across
  every map, no retyping. Manage the library from the same panel.

Roster characters become tokens
- Press + Add under a node's Characters and a token appears on the board at
  once — a stand-in circle showing the character's initials, placed on the
  shape linked to that room (or at the map's home point). Rename the
  character and the token follows; set a portrait and the token wears it;
  remove the character and its stand-in goes too (player-owned tokens and
  tokens with a sheet attached are left alone). Hovering the token shows the
  roster notes to the GM only.

Faster map building
- Set a room image on a data-map node and Waypoint builds a child map from
  it: the picture becomes the scene on that map's play map (locked, at the
  back), and the node links to it as a portal — double-click to visit.
- Double-click a map or planner name in the sidebar to rename it.

Help where you're looking
- Hover any section heading or field label — Categories, Characters, Linked
  Map, Layer, the Host and Join panels, Settings sections, the pen and eraser
  menus, and more — for a one-line explanation of what it's for.

Settings
- Coordinate rulers can be hidden (Settings → Table).
- Grid opacity slider — a personal setting: in multiplayer each player fades
  the grid to taste without affecting anyone else.

WAYPOINT 1.1.0
============================

Multiplayer (new)
- Host your campaign as the GM and have players join with a room code —
  direct peer-to-peer between your computers, no accounts, no server.
- Players follow your map, move only the tokens you assign to them, and
  talk in table chat (with private GM whispers).
- Hide any item behind a grey cloud until you choose to reveal it. Your
  planners, room notes, and character dossiers are never sent to players.
- Split the party through doors and stairs; Summon All brings everyone back.
- Pause the Table freezes players completely while chat stays open.
- The Host panel shows exactly which campaign and map players will join —
  and lets you change both.
- Set a display name and table picture in Settings; they show in the roster.
- If two networks can't connect directly, traffic falls back to an encrypted
  public relay automatically — most "it won't connect" cases just work now.
- Table security: first-time joiners need the GM's approval, an optional
  session password can back up the room code, and the GM can kick a player
  (kicked or denied players stay out for the rest of the session).

Play Map
- Copy, cut, and paste items (Ctrl+C / X / V) — and paste images straight
  from your clipboard onto the board.
- A floating toolbar over your selection: color, layer, lock, hide, delete.
- Align and distribute buttons for multi-selections; arrow-key nudging.
- Per-item opacity, plus a default opacity for newly added items.
- Square and hex grids per map with grid-aware snapping; text stays legible
  above the grid, and any item can join it via right-click.
- Measure tool with Imperial/Metric units and adjustable per-map scale.
- Image Library: browse every image in your install and place it in a click.

Getting around
- Minimap in the top-left corner — click anywhere on it to fly there.
- Ctrl+K quick-jump to any map, planner, or room by name.
- Resizable side panels, and a rebuilt Help full of step-by-step tutorials.

Exports & data safety
- Export This Map / All Maps / All Play Maps / All Planners / Everything —
  images come bundled in a .zip, and Import accepts those bundles whole.
- Saves are written atomically, and every launch snapshots your save into
  saves\backups (newest 10 kept).
- Saves from ANY older Waypoint version are upgraded automatically on first
  launch — the original is backed up first and never silently overwritten.

Character sheets (shadow-base.com)
- Exports follow the site's published contract: the languages line is the
  bracket-free projection of the entries (no more double-billed tongues or
  the "languages are structured rows now" notice on every load), familiarity
  and literacy lists use semicolons, and Size Modifier is always written.
- Import Character (toolbar) turns a ShadowBase character JSON into a ready
  token: the sheet's portrait becomes the token art, name and a stats line
  fill in, and the full sheet rides attached.
- View (inspector) opens the attached sheet read-only — attributes,
  characteristics, traits, skills, Force powers, and inventory at the table.
- Export JSON writes a site-ready file back out with the sharpest available
  portrait embedded — upload it on the site with Import Character. Tokens
  without a sheet export a starter file the site fills with defaults.
