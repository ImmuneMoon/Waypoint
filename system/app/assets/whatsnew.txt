WAYPOINT 1.4.6 — What's New
============================

Tokens
- Elevation and posture. Two new switches in Settings → Table give every
  character token a height in yards (a ledge at +3, a catwalk at +5, a pit
  at −2) and a posture — standing, crouching, sitting, kneeling, crawling,
  lying prone or lying face up (handbook ch. 9). Both show as small chips at
  the foot of the token, in its hover card and in Properties; the token's
  right-click menu edits them too. Players set their own token's by
  right-clicking it. Both switches are on from the first launch; a table
  that runs flat switches them off once and the choice sticks. Switching
  one off hides the chips but keeps the values, and in a session the GM's
  switches decide what players see. The switches travel with the saves
  folder like the other table settings.
- An attached ShadowBase sheet seeds the token's posture and elevation
  from its details, and Export JSON writes both back into the sheet, using
  the website's posture ids (lying-prone, lying-face-up).

Blast tool
- A new 💥 Blast tool next to Measure drops a grenade's area effect on a
  cell: Frag 12 ft, Concussion 12 ft, Sonic 20 ft, Ion 24 ft, Plasma 30 ft,
  or any radius in feet (÷3 = yards on the map). Every token within the
  radius lights up with its distance in yards, so the GM applies the
  handbook's damage ÷ (3 × distance) by hand — Waypoint never rolls.
  Distance is hex distance; with Token elevation on it is straight-line,
  height included (√(h² + v²)), from the blast's own height — the token in
  the landing cell, else the ground, editable in the tool's menu. Drag a
  blast to move it, right-click it to remove it. Blasts are local, like
  rulers.
- Rulers: with Token elevation on, a ruler between two tokens at different
  heights prints the 3D figure as a second number.

Maps
- New Parent Map: right-click a map in the sidebar to wrap it in a new map.
  The existing map moves under the new one (taking the same place in the
  tree the old one had), and the new map's data map starts with a portal
  node that leads into it. Planners get the same: New Parent Planner.

Data maps
- Line types. Link Mode now opens a menu to pick the type of the lines you
  are about to draw: a solid Path, a dashed Route, a dotted Secret way, or a
  One-way arrow from the first room you click to the second. Every line
  carries a small chip at its middle: click it, in any tool, to change the
  line type or remove the link. Done linking (or the Select tool) leaves
  Link Mode.
- Lines have Properties. Click a line (or its chip) and the right panel
  shows its type, a label that is drawn on the line for everyone to see,
  GM-only notes about the route, a swap for its direction, and Remove.
  Delete removes the selected link. Right-click the chip for the quick
  type menu.

Tutorial
- Help now has a Tutorial tab. Start it and Waypoint adds a small campaign
  called Tutorial to your save (a valley, a cave nested under it with
  tokens, and a session plan), then walks you through the app in about
  fifteen steps with a spotlight over the real controls: campaigns, the map
  tree, both faces of a map, linking, portals, the play map tools and
  tokens, planners, multiplayer, settings. Esc leaves it at any time. The
  Tutorial campaign is yours to keep and build on, or to discard from the
  same tab (or at the end of the tour); it can be rebuilt fresh any time.
  On a brand-new save the tour starts by itself, once; everyone else
  finds it under Help → Tutorial.
- Help also has a Coming up tab: the intended features on record (character
  sheets in the app, blasts thrown from the sheet with per-campaign,
  customizable presets, lighter multiplayer sync), kept with each release.

Play map
- Hovering an item that is linked to a room now shows the room's notes on the
  card as well (for the GM only; players still never receive notes).

Text boxes
- With the Text tool armed, clicking an existing text box edits it in place
  (the caret lands at the end) instead of dropping a new box on top of it.
  Double-click still works in the Select tool.
- While a text box is being edited the caret can be placed anywhere in it,
  including the space above and below the words, and text can be
  highlighted; the box goes back to its centred layout when you click away.
- Text Opacity and Background Opacity are separate sliders in Properties, so
  a box can have solid words on a faint plate (or the other way round). The
  Opacity slider further down still fades the whole box.

Multiplayer
- Per-change sync. The GM's table now sends only what changed on a map —
  the tokens, shapes, rooms or links that moved, appeared or went away —
  instead of the whole map every time anything on it changed. Busy boards
  with many tokens stay snappy over the wire; a whole-map copy is still
  sent whenever that is the smaller message.

Fixes
- Grouped items no longer drift apart when moved. On release the grid
  snap (and, on hex maps, the seating of hex items into their cell) moved
  only the item under the pointer; every item in the drag now takes the
  same correction, so a text box grouped with a teleport point keeps its
  place beside it no matter which of the two you drag.
- Switching campaigns from the header dropdown (and some imports) threw
  an error trying to press a centre button that no longer exists; the map
  now simply opens on its remembered camera.

WAYPOINT 1.3.6 — What's New
============================

Combat
- Turn order at the table. Start Combat from the play-map right-click menu
  (Session) or the Multiplayer panel: a roster of everyone on that map — the
  party first, every other character below, plus custom rows for things
  without a token — with an initiative each. Only the tokens in a live
  target pair on that map come ticked (whoever is targeting and whoever
  they target); everything else waits for your tick. Higher goes first, ties keep
  their place, and you can drag rows into any order by hand.
- Running it: Next and Previous turn, a round counter, and End Combat from
  the same menu or the turn strip at the top of the map. The active token
  gets a gold ring for everyone. Players see the strip — whose turn it is and
  who is next — and cannot change it.
- A player targeting an NPC asks the GM whether to start combat there (once
  per NPC per session). Nothing starts on its own.
- One combat per map, so a split party can fight on two maps at once. Late
  joiners see the running combat; it ends with the session.

Table notepad
- A disposable notepad for the table. The GM opens it from the play-map
  right-click menu (Session) or the Multiplayer panel; everyone sees it as
  the GM types. Anyone can save it to their Journal with one press; the GM
  can clear it (it asks first). It is gone when the GM puts it away or the
  session ends — never part of the save.


Planners
- Scene status. A Status picker in the planner toolbar marks a scene Next,
  Played or Skipped; the mark shows before its name in the Planners tree,
  and only one scene is Next at a time. The play-map right-click menu shows
  "Next scene: <name>" and opens it.
- Planner-to-map links. A Scene node can point at a map, and a room on it;
  the rendered preview shows an "Open <map> · <room>" link that jumps there.

Maps
- Pinned and Recent maps sit above the Maps tree: pin a map from the play-map
  right-click menu, and the last five maps you opened are always one click
  away. Recent is kept per campaign on this computer.
- Hovering a portal names the map it leads to and the room you land in.

Session log
- Everything that happens at the table is written to a log on the campaign:
  session start and end, who joined and left, every handout shown, every
  crossing, table-wide chat, shares, pauses and travel locks. Open it from
  the Multiplayer panel or the play-map right-click menu, filter by kind,
  and save it as a text file — the raw material for the next recap. GM data,

Handouts
- Tags. Give a handout tags (faces, places, letters — anything, comma
  separated); a tag row at the top of the Handouts panel filters the list.
  Tags travel with the handout: players see them as chips in their Inbox,
  a click on a chip searches the journal for it, and the smart search
  matches them.

At the table

  your map lit, players who left dimmed. Click a face to find that character
  on your map, or to hear which map they are on; right-click to target.
  It is read-only for players — no dragging, no summoning.

Snapshots
- Settings ▸ Advanced lists the save's snapshots: the copy Waypoint takes
  at every launch (newest ten kept) and any you take by hand with
  "Snapshot the Save Now" (kept until deleted). Restore puts the whole save
  back to that copy and reloads — the save as it was is snapshotted first.
  Needs this release's installer; older shells still get the launch backups.

Faster maps
- Pictures on a play map decode off the main thread and load as they scroll
  into view, so a map full of art opens sooner. Players on a map the GM is
  not on still get its pictures the same way as before.

Fixes

Look

Appearance

  system delay, in a panel that follows the theme.

Multiplayer
- Lock Travel Between Maps, beside Pause in the Multiplayer panel. While it
  is on, players stay on the maps they are on — portals, warps and room links
  do nothing for them — but the table is not paused: tokens still move and
  chat stays open. Players see a small banner and a note if they try. Late
  joiners inherit the lock; it clears when the session ends.
- Right-click on empty space of a play map during a session for a Session
  menu: for the GM, Summon Everyone Here, Lock / Allow Travel, Pause / Resume
  and End Session, plus "Bring <name> here" for every player the campaign

  Leave). Between sessions the same right-click offers "Bring <name> here":
  it puts that player's token on this map at the click point (moving the

Campaign Cast
- Save a character once, drop copies anywhere. Right-click a character token
  and choose Save to Campaign Cast; from then on the empty-space right-click
  on any play map lists the cast — click a name for one copy at that spot,
  or ×5 for five (numbered). Manage Cast renames or removes entries; tokens
  already placed are not affected. The cast is GM data and never reaches
  players. The cast also sits at the top of the image library, so creating a
  token starts with a choice of saved characters: click one to place a copy
  at the centre of your view, or its ×5 badge for five.

Portals
- Tokens no longer pile up on a portal. Whoever arrives takes the nearest
  free spot beside the landing point, and the token left behind on the map
  they came from steps off the portal, so the next crossing is not blocked
  and the GM no longer has to unstack anyone. A token that was already on
  the destination stays where the GM put it — unless it is still sitting on
  the landing node, in which case it is nudged to the nearest free spot.
- NPC tokens travel too, the same way players do: drag one onto a portal
  and the destination gets a copy of it (only if it has none already, placed
  beside the landing point); the token left behind stays in its room, steps
  off the portal and is hidden from players. Works with or without a session
  running.
- The GM can walk a player's character through a portal between sessions,
  or while that player is away from the table: their copy on the other map
  is placed beside the landing point (or left alone if it is already there
  and off the node), and the token left behind steps off the portal.
- A player who leaves the table stays visible where they were: their token
  on the map they left from remains, and copies of theirs on every other map
  are hidden from the players until they are back. The GM's leave notice
  names the map.

Fixes
- The Select tool on the play map toolbar uses the same angled arrow as the
  data map, instead of an upright one.
