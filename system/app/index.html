Help & Tutorials

What Waypoint is

Waypoint organizes a tabletop campaign into Maps (locations with two faces: a node-based Data Map for your notes and a freeform Play Map for the battle map) and Planners (document pages for session plans). Everything lives in the left sidebar.

New here? The 🎓 Tutorial tab runs a guided tour over a small demo campaign that you can keep and build on — or type in the search box above to jump straight to any topic.

The welcome screen

When Waypoint opens you land on a Welcome screen: Start a campaign, Import one from a file, Continue a campaign you already have, or Join a game. Join a game opens its own screen where you set your name, color and picture (your profile, which follows you to every table) and enter the GM’s room code — it stays up showing the connection and drops you in once the GM’s table loads. Click the Waypoint logo (top-left) to bring the Welcome screen back any time, set your profile any time under Settings ▸ Profile, and choose how often the screen appears under Settings ▸ Profile ▸ Welcome screen on launch (every time, first run only, or never).

Your first map

  1. Press + next to Maps in the left sidebar and name it.
  2. The Properties sidebar on the right opens when you select a room or an item and closes when the selection clears; the ▶ button on its edge opens or closes it by hand.
  3. Use the Data Map / Play Map switch at the top to flip between the two faces of the same location.
  4. Drag one map onto another in the sidebar to nest it inside (world → city → building). A breadcrumb appears up top so you always know where you are. Right-click a map for New Child Map (a map inside it) or New Parent Map (a new map that wraps it — the existing map moves under it and the new map gets a portal node leading in). To re-order siblings, drop a map on the top or bottom third of another (a gold line shows where it lands), or right-click → ↑ Move Up / ↓ Move Down — the moved map brings any nested maps along, and the order is saved with the campaign.

Getting around fast

  • Ctrl + K opens quick-jump: type any map, planner, or room name and hit Enter to go straight there.
  • The minimap (top-left corner) shows where your content is — click anywhere on it to fly the camera there. It dims when idle and the chevron collapses it.
  • Drag the thin gutters beside either sidebar to resize the panels.

Saving & backups

  • Everything autosaves to saves/data.json a moment after each change — there is no save button to forget.
  • To back up or move your campaign, copy the saves folder. That's the whole thing.
  • Export As makes shareable files at any scope: just the open map or planner, all maps / play maps / planners in the campaign, or everything. Exports that use images arrive as a .zip with the pictures bundled in; Import accepts those zips (or plain .json) and merges by id or replaces.
Waypoint works fully offline. Internet is only needed for Multiplayer and for diagram blocks in Planners.

Interactive tutorial

The tour is a guided walkthrough over the real app — campaigns and the map tree, both faces of a map, the toolbars, tokens, planners, multiplayer and settings — in short steps grouped into sections you can jump between. Nothing is locked while it runs: try each thing as the card describes it, then press Next. Every card shows its section and a ▾ menu to skip to another. Esc leaves the tour at any point.

Or jump straight to a section:
The Tutorial campaign is a normal campaign in your save: rename it, add to it, host a session on it. Discarding it deletes only that campaign.

Tools

  • ➚ Select / Move (default) — click rooms to edit them, drag to move, drag a box to select several.
  • ✋ Pan — drag anywhere to move the map itself; rooms never move while it's active. (Middle-click drag — or hold Space and drag — pans in any tool.)
  • ↔ Link Mode — lit gold while active; click two rooms to connect them. Its menu picks the line type for new links: a solid Path, a dashed Route, a dotted Secret way, or a One-way arrow from the first room to the second. Done linking (or the Select tool) leaves it.
  • 🗑 Clear the board — removes every room and link on this map after a confirmation (this map's Undo brings them back).

Rooms (nodes)

  1. Click ➕ Add Room, then drag the node where you want it.
  2. Select it and fill in the right sidebar: name, category color, notes, and the characters found there.
  3. Set Room Image does double duty: the picture shows on hover, and Waypoint builds a child map with that image as the scene, linking the node to it as a portal. (Nodes that already lead somewhere just get the picture.)
  4. Double Click a map or planner name in the sidebar to rename it.
  5. Categories are per-map — add your own under Categories. Press ☆ on one (or Save all to library) and any other map can add it from the Category Library with the same name and color.
  6. + Add under Characters also drops a stand-in token on the play map (initials in a circle); set a portrait and the token wears it, rename the character and the token follows.

Connections

  1. Click ↔ Link Mode, then click two rooms one after the other to connect them.
  2. Hover a line and a small chip appears at its middle showing its type (a labelled line keeps its chip, with the label on it). Click a line or its chip to open it in Properties: the line type, a label drawn on the line (players see it), GM-only notes about the route (distance, danger, what it takes to find it), a swap for the direction, and Remove. Delete removes the selected link; right-click the chip for a quick type menu.

Linked maps (doors, stairs, warps)

  1. Select a room and set its Linked Map to another map, plus an icon (door, stairs, gate…).
  2. Double Click the room — or any play map item tied to it — to travel to that map. Use the breadcrumb to climb back out. In multiplayer, a player travels by dropping their token on the portal item (double-click works too), and the GM can send a player through by dropping that player's token there.

GM notes on the play map

Select a play map item, link it to a room in the right sidebar, and press Generate GM Notes to build a styled note card from that room's notes and characters. Hover a linked item to preview the room's info.

Placing things

  • Pick a shape or T text from the Shapes menu, then click the board to drop it there — or drag to draw exactly the box it should fill (hexagons resize like any other shape). Esc cancels. The New shape size switch in that menu picks Free (default size on click, exact box on drag) or Grid cell (every new shape arrives exactly one cell in size and seats into the cell — handy for hex tokens and tiles). 🖼️ adds an image, or just drag an image file from your computer onto the board.
  • Text boxes: Double Click on the canvas to edit the words, or select the box and type in the Content editor in Properties — the same rich-text toolbar as a planner's text block (bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, bulleted and numbered lists, clear formatting, and the symbol tray), with your edits shown on the canvas as you type. The color swatch (toolbar or Properties) sets the text color, and Text Alignment in Properties places the words horizontally and vertically within the box.
  • 🗄️ Image Library shows this campaign's pictures by default — every picture uploaded from its maps, planners and pages, used by them, or brought in — with Shared (the tutorial art), Unfiled (pictures whose map, planner or page is gone) and All campaigns a chip away. Import from another campaign… lets you pick pictures from another campaign, Shared or Unfiled (Ctrl-click several) and bring them in: nothing is copied, this campaign simply remembers them. Categories are per campaign (right-click a picture to tag it, + New for a new one, the tools on a chip rename, shelve or delete it); Make shared turns one into a category every campaign sees, marked ◇. Click a picture for a large preview, then Add to map.
  • 🎵 Sound (GM only) opens a small panel that stays open while you run the scene: Ambient loops as chips (click one to crossfade to it, ■ fades it out) and Cues as buttons (click to fire once; up to four overlap). Every player at the table hears what you play at your Master volume; Mute mine silences your own speakers only. Library… holds this campaign's sounds — Add sounds… uploads MP3, OGG, M4A or WebM files (4 MB each; loops up to two minutes, cues cut after thirty seconds; OGG loops cleanly, MP3 clicks at the seam), each with a name, a Loop / Cue switch, a gain, a preview and Delete; Shared is the bundled set — seven ambient loops (rain, forest, dungeon, cavern, night crickets, wind, campfire) and twelve cues (door opens / closes, latch, coins, footsteps, page flip, blade drawn, creak, dice, sword clash, healing, teleport), all CC0 and credited in assets/sounds/NOTICES.txt — hidden per install rather than deleted (Show hidden brings one back); Import from another campaign… brings another campaign's sounds in by reference, nothing copied. Files live in saves/images/audio/ and travel to each player over the connection one at a time, the playing loop first, so a player who joins mid-scene hears the loop once its file has crossed. Sound is a VTT feature (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features): off for the campaign, nothing plays at the table. Everyone has the 🔊 speaker in the Table pill for their own volume and mute; in a browser tab, one click on the page is needed before it may play sound.
  • Ctrl + C / Ctrl + X / Ctrl + V copy, cut, and paste selected items; pasting a screenshot or copied image from outside the app drops it at your cursor.
  • The New item opacity slider in the Shapes and Pen menus sets the transparency new items are created with.
  • Click to select; drag the corner handle to resize (hold Shift — or turn on Lock proportions in the selection toolbar — to keep the width/height ratio; resizing one member of a group scales the whole group); drag the blue dot to rotate. Arrow keys nudge the selection (Shift for bigger steps).
  • ✏️ Draw sketches freehand (click it again for color, stroke size, pen tip — round, square, or a flat calligraphy nib whose width varies with direction — straight-line mode, and opacity). 🧽 Eraser rubs out only what its circle passes over — the rest of the stroke stays. Click the eraser again for its size and mode: Precise trims strokes exactly to the circle, Segments removes whole point-to-point pieces.
  • 🪣 Fill (GM) colors grid cells: click or drag cells to lay a cell-sized colored shape (a hexagon on hex maps, a square on square maps) beneath the tokens; right-click a cell to clear it, and painting a filled cell recolors it. Each fill is an ordinary board item — move it, restyle it, change its opacity or delete it like any shape.
  • 🎯 Center menu: jump to the canvas center, to all items, Fit to Content (Shift+1 — zoom-and-pan so everything, or just the selection, fits the screen), or Center on Character — your own token in multiplayer, or the selected one as GM.
  • Drop a ⚡ Trigger Zone and give it an Event Message — it fires when a character token is dragged inside. The button picks a shape to fit the grid (hex on hex maps, square on square grids); change it any time under Trigger Shape.
  • Any item can be a portal: Properties → Portal to Map. On a nested map the parent is offered first (⬆ Back to…), so a way home takes one click — no node needed.
  • Trap tiles: a hidden portal (right-click → Hide from Players) is normally inert — a decorative or unfinished way. Tick ⚠️ Trap in its Properties to arm it: players never see the tile, but a token that lands on it is still teleported. You see a red ⚠️ and a dashed outline on an armed trap; a locked destination map still stops it, so you stay in control of when it can fire.

The selection toolbar

A small toolbar floats above whatever you select: color, layer up/down, group/ungroup (🔗 — lit gold when the selection is a group), lock proportions (the dashed box with a diagonal arrow — a padlock appears on it when on), lock (the padlock shows the current state: closed = locked), player visibility, duplicate (⧉ — a full copy with every setting and attached data; also Ctrl + D, the right-click menu, and Properties), delete. Select several items and right-click for Align and Space (distribute) buttons.

Who can see this? Every item is either 👁 Visible to players or 🚫 Hidden from players. The big box at the top of the Properties panel shows which, and the toolbar's eye turns red when hidden. Hidden items show to you dimmed; players at your table see nothing at all. Use it for secret doors, unrevealed enemies, and GM-only notes.

The right-click menu

  • Lock / Unlock — locked items can't be moved or resized (they dim slightly).
  • Hide from Players / Show to Players — in multiplayer, hidden items appear to players as a grey cloud; you see a ghost with an eye.
  • Show Above Grid — lifts an item above the grid lines (text does this automatically).
  • Bring to Front / Forward / Backward / Send to Back — five stacking layers for building scenes.
  • Opacity slider — adjust transparency of anything already on the board.
  • Select several items (drag a box around them) to Group them or Merge Drawings.
Character tokens: any shape or image with Is Character set in the right sidebar becomes a token with a name and stats tooltip — and can be handed to a player in multiplayer. Tokens pair with shadow-base.com character sheets: the 🤵 Import Character toolbar button turns a site JSON into a ready token (portrait as art, sheet attached), the inspector's View opens the full read-only sheet (stats, traits, skills, inventory), and Export JSON writes a site-ready file back out — no sheet attached still exports a starter file.
Toolbar groups: to keep the play-map toolbar compact, the content tools live under the + Add button (text box, image, the picture library, character import) and the GM ambience tools under the 🎬 Scene button (sound, visual effects). Opening any toolbar menu closes the others, so only one is ever up.

Fog of war

The 🌫 Fog tool (GM only) hides the play map behind an opaque fog and reveals only what a character can see. Fog is a VTT feature, per campaign (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features) and is on by default, so the 🌫 button is on the toolbar; switch the feature off there if you never use it. There is no player switch — when you run fog, everyone at the table plays with it.

Walls & pillars that block sight: flag a fill cell or a shape (rectangle, hexagon, circle/pillar or diamond) as Blocks sight in its Properties panel and it stops sight — a token’s vision (and each player’s view) ends at it instead of seeing through. Occlusion works on square and hex maps; a manual Reveal still overrides it. Set the type to Door to make it openable: closed it blocks, open it lets sight through — toggle a door from its Properties or by clicking it while in fog mode, and a player can open/close a door their token stands next to (unless you lock it). (A height dimension is coming; today a blocker blocks fully regardless of height.)

Cover: the same blockers grant cover. Turn on Cover for your system (⚙ Settings ▸ System ▸ Items, under the combat settings) and drag the 📏 ruler between two character tokens — a line reports the cover between them (half / three-quarters / total, or just “cover” for systems that grade it once). Cover is read from the walls, pillars, closed doors and filled cells on the map and works on square and hex. It is advisory — Waypoint estimates cover from the map’s blockers (whole-cell, token size approximated) and you make the call; it changes no dice or damage. Fog need not be on for cover to show.

  • Turn fog on for this map — open the 🌫 menu and tick Fog on this map. Fog is set per map, so a lit tavern and a dark dungeon can live in the same campaign. To do a whole campaign at once, press 🌫 Fog on · all maps; tick New maps in this campaign start with fog on so maps you add later begin fogged too.
  • Play areas — fog only where you want it — mark an image or shape as a play area (its Properties, or the ▣ button on the selection toolbar) and fog covers only the cells under marked items — scenes, backdrops and map art around them stay lit, so you never hand-clear them. On a map where nothing is marked, the When no play area is marked choice decides: Fog the whole map (the classic behaviour) or No fog until you mark one. Hidden items never fog. This is the tidy way to fog just the battlemap on a page full of art.
  • How far a token sees — set a Default sight range in yards for a token with no character (or an unmapped field), and optionally Sight from a character field: pick a field of your system (Help ▸ Character sheets) and each character sees as far as its own value. On a gridless map, choose Square (radius vision) or Hex (front-arc vision) and the pixels per cell, so Waypoint can measure sight without drawing a grid.
  • All around or a facing cone — set Vision to All around (a token sees in every direction — the default on square maps) or Facing cone (it sees only ahead, and the cone turns as the token turns — the default on hex maps). Vision is set per map and independent of the grid, so a square map can use a cone and a hex map can see all around; with a cone you also pick its width in degrees (90° a torch, 180° a wide arc). Tick Use this vision for new maps to make it the starting choice for maps you create later — existing maps are left exactly as they are.
  • Paint it by hand — the 👁 Reveal and 🌫 Hide brushes click or drag over the map to carve out or cover cells (right-click paints the other brush). Reveal all lights the whole scene; Cover all hides everything except what you reveal by hand.
  • Preview a player’s view — the Preview a player’s view picker drops the fog onto your own screen as one player — or the whole party — would see it, so you can check a reveal before you make it. Set it back to Off — whole board to see everything again.
What players see: each player’s screen is covered by an opaque fog of their own token’s vision — only the cells within sight show, and a creature they cannot see is not drawn at all. Your machine works out each player’s vision and sends every player only what they may see, so nothing hidden crosses the wire. Fog has no per-player switch; the GM runs it for the whole table.

Grids

  1. Click the grid button in the play map toolbar to cycle Off → Square → Hex. Hex cells are flat-top (a flat side faces up). The choice is remembered per map.
  2. Grids draw on top of your art so cells stay visible; text lifts above the grid automatically, and any other item can via right-click → Show Above Grid.
  3. Turn on 🧲 Snap and tokens seat themselves into cells as you drop them. On hex maps, character tokens always center into cells, and freehand strokes follow cell corners even on fast swipes.
  4. Tokens follow rooms: link a floor shape to a room (Properties → Link Node) and any character token dropped onto it moves into that room's character list automatically — the data map always knows who is where.
  5. Snap To (click the Snap button again) picks what a drag aligns to: Grid — cells only, the most predictable; Items — neighbours only, for tidy rows; Both — neighbours first, grid for open-space drops. Remembered per computer.
  6. Tokens turn in cell-side steps while a grid is on: 4 facings on a square grid, 6 on a hex grid (one per face — hex cells are flat-top, so straight up is a face). Click a selected token's arrow to turn it one facing clockwise (Shift-click = counter-clockwise), or drag the arrow to aim it; the blue rotate dot works too. Hold Shift while turning for free 15° steps. Properties → Turning chooses whether the art turns with the arrow or the arrow alone moves around an upright picture. With no grid they turn freely; applying a grid turns every token to its nearest facing. A small gold arrow marks each token's front and always points across a cell face at a neighbouring cell — pick which side of the art that is under Properties → Front Side. Players can turn their own token. Token facing is a per-campaign VTT feature (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features), on by default and GM-controlled like fog: switch it off and tokens lose the turn arrow, and a map set to a facing cone falls back to all-around vision (nothing can aim the cone).
  7. Token condition: right-click a token (or use Properties → Condition) for Alive, Incapacitated (red X over the token) or Dead (skull, art darkened). Players see it too.
  8. In Items or Both mode, Snap makes item edges magnetic: drag an image next to another and it glues flush and falls into line automatically — rows and columns without pixel-hunting. Item-to-item snaps win over the grid; drop something in open space and the grid takes it.
  9. Resizing matches sizes too (Snap on or off): pull a corner near another item's width or height and it pauses there with a ↔ / ↕ badge — ease past to break free, release to keep the match. With proportions locked, the other side follows.
  10. ⊞ Fit to Grid (selection toolbar or Properties) sizes the selection to whole cells and seats it — tokens and hex shapes become exactly one hex cell.

Measuring

  1. Pick the 📏 Measure tool and drag across the map to place a ruler; click the tool again for options.
  2. Choose Imperial or Metric. By default a hex cell is 1 yard across and a square is 5 feet.
  3. For huge maps (space battles!), set Map Scale in the measure options — e.g. one cell = 100 yd or 2 km. Each map keeps its own scale.
  4. Click any placed ruler to remove it (it turns red on hover); Clear rulers in the measure options removes them all.
  5. With Token elevation on for the campaign (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features), a ruler between two tokens at different heights prints the straight-line 3D figure as a second number.

Blast radius

The 💥 Blast tool drops a grenade's area effect on a cell: pick the weapon (Frag 12 ft, Ion 24 ft… or type any radius in feet; ÷3 = yards) and click the map. Every token within the radius lights up with its distance in yards, so you apply the handbook's damage ÷ (3 × distance) by hand — Waypoint never rolls for you. Distance is hex distance; with Token elevation on it is straight-line, height included (√(h² + v²)), from the blast's own height (the token in the landing cell, else the ground — editable in the tool's menu). Drag a blast to move it (it re-seats in a cell and, unless you set its height, takes the height of whoever stands there); right-click it to remove it. Blasts are yours alone, like rulers.

💥 Boom: with Visual effects on, each blast template shows a Boom label — click it to set off an explosion burst at that spot for everyone on the map.

Blasts from the sheet: this toolbar 💥 is now a GM quick-tool for an ad-hoc radius. Per-item blasts are thrown by the character — PC or NPC — from their sheet, using the campaign’s own item library (System ▸ Items), each item with its own name, radius and damage. See Help ▸ Character sheets.

Elevation & posture

Two VTT features in ⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features give every character token a height in yards and a posture (standing, crouching, sitting, kneeling, crawling, prone, face up). Both show as small chips at the foot of the token and in its Properties; players right-click their own token to set theirs. Switch either off and the chips vanish but the values stay. They are set per campaign; in a session your campaign's settings are the most your players see.

VTT settings

  • Per campaign. Token elevation, token posture, token facing, the minimap, sound, dice, character sheets, visual effects and fog of war are switched in ⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features for the campaign on screen, and saved with it in the save file — a restore or an import brings a campaign's settings back with it. VTT integration is the master switch: off is the plain whiteboard, every feature off and hidden, with the per-feature choices kept for when it goes back on.
  • Default for new campaigns. The same section holds the default a campaign starts with. Changing it touches no existing campaign; Apply to existing campaigns… copies it onto the campaigns you tick (nothing is ticked to begin with). The default lives with your preferences (the saves folder's preferences.json) and is cleared by Reset Local Preferences; the campaigns are not.
  • In a session, the GM's campaign is the ceiling. Players see at most what the hosted campaign has on. A GM change mid-session reaches the table at once, with a short notice on each player's screen.
  • Off for me. At a table, a player can switch a feature the GM has on off for themselves in the same section (“Turn off for me”), never on when the GM has it off. The choice is remembered per table (that campaign, that GM) and never touches the player's own default or campaigns.
  • The join notice. On joining a table whose settings differ from your own default, one window lists what is on at the table but off for you (your own default carries in, so those start off for you: Use the table's settings turns them on for you there, Keep mine leaves them off) and what the GM has off and hidden while you are there. It shows again only when the GM has changed the settings since you last saw them.
  • One campaign per session. While hosting, anything that would put another campaign on screen — the campaign picker, a search result, Ctrl + K, New or Delete Campaign, Import Replace, the tutorial — first asks, and on yes ends the session for everyone before switching.

Coordinate rulers & minimap

The number strips along the top and left edges are coordinate rulers. Turn them off (or back on) in ⚙ Settings ▸ Table, alongside a Grid opacity slider — both are yours alone, so in multiplayer every player can fade the grid to taste without changing anyone else's view. The minimap in the corner is a VTT feature (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features, per campaign); the ▾ on the box itself only folds it away and is yours alone. Zoom steps are 5% per click or Mouse Wheel tick (the wheel zooms toward the pointer; hold Shift to scroll instead).

Building pages

  1. Press + next to Planners and open your new page.
  2. Use the planner toolbar to add blocks: headings, prose, callouts, tables, and flowcharts. Blocks can be reordered and edited in place.
  3. Planners nest under planners just like maps, and re-order the same way — drag onto the top or bottom of a row, or right-click → ↑ Move Up / ↓ Move Down. Keep one parent per module with a child page per scene.

Undo and redo

  • Every planner keeps its own history: the ↩ ↪ buttons in the planner toolbar take back the last edit on this page — a deleted block, row or node comes back where it was, at the same scroll position — and never touch another planner or a map.
  • Inside a text field, Ctrl + Z is first the browser's own text undo (your typing, character by character). Once that field has nothing left to take back — nothing typed in it since the page was last rebuilt — the same key reaches the planner's history. Outside a field it always does.
  • Not undoable: renaming, re-ordering, re-parenting, the scene status, creating a page, and deleting one. Deleting takes a safety copy of your save first (Settings → Advanced → Snapshots), so nothing is lost for good.
  • How far back: Undo keeps the last 100 steps per map and per planner by default; set it from 10 to 500 in Settings → Advanced → Undo history size. Deeper history uses more memory on this computer.

Import and export

A planner can come in from a Markdown file — the import button in the planner toolbar appends to the open planner, the file button beside + in the Planners header makes a new one, and the header Import button takes a .md too — with a preview of every block before anything is written; ### Scene: Name opens a scene node. Save Markdown on the Export menu writes it back out. The format is under Handbook › Import and export.

Diagram blocks render with an internet connection. In multiplayer, planners are never sent to players — they are yours alone. For pages your players can read, see Handbook.

Appearance

The 🎨 button in the planner toolbar gives the open page an optional font, text color and background color. Each is an override, so anything you leave alone keeps the app’s own style, and Reset clears them. Use this page’s look as the default makes it the campaign default — inherited by every planner, Handbook page and character sheet that has no look of its own, and sent to your players’ Handbook so their pages match yours (fonts come from a fixed list and colors are plain, so a themed page can never carry anything unsafe to a player). Clear default removes it.

What a page is

A Handbook page is a rules or reference document for your table — house rules, a quick-reference sheet, a primer on the setting — written in the same block editor as a planner and nested and re-ordered the same way. The difference is who reads it: players at your table receive the pages you leave open to them and read them from a Handbook tree of their own, in a panel over the play map, while the session keeps running underneath.

  1. Press + next to Handbook in the left panel and name the page.
  2. Add blocks from + Add Block…: title, sections and sub-headings, prose, lead text, callouts, pictures, plain tables, a horizontal line, flowcharts and diagrams. Everything on a page is player-safe by design — there are no scene nodes, raw HTML or GM notes on a page; keep those in planners.
  3. The preview on the right shows exactly what a player gets. Double-click a block in it to jump to that block in the editor.

Players can read / GM only

  • Every page has a switch in the planner toolbar — 👁 Players can read (the default) or 🔒 GM only — and the same choice by right-clicking the page in the tree. A GM-only page shows a small lock in the tree and never leaves your machine.
  • While hosting, turning a page GM only removes it from every player at once (their reader closes if it was open); turning it back on sends it again. Edits to an open page reach players as you type.
  • Only the pages of the campaign you are hosting reach the table. Your other campaigns' pages stay home whatever their switch says.

Layout

A page flows like a web page, not a fixed sheet: a player's narrower window, the print path and the HTML export all reflow the same content. The layout row under a picture, callout, flare, table, diagram or flowchart sets its width (a quarter to the full column), whether it floats left or right with the text wrapping around it, a nudge in pixels from where the flow put it, and span all columns inside a multi-column section. A Section (H2) block takes 1, 2 or 3 columns for everything under it up to the next section or title. In the preview, drag a picture to nudge it and drag its bottom-right corner to resize it; each drag is one undo step.

  • Spanning all columns keeps a block in the flow, so the float control switches off while span is on.
  • Inside a two- or three-column section a callout or flare cannot float and keeps at least half the column: a third of a narrow column is a few dozen pixels.
  • A large upward nudge on a floated picture can overlap the text above it; the preview shows exactly what players get, so look before you leave it.

Import and export

A page or a planner can come in from a Markdown file and go back out as one. Three ways in: the import button in the planner toolbar appends to the open document; the file button beside + in the Planners and Handbook headers makes a new one; the header Import button takes a .md (or a zip that holds one) and asks which it becomes. What players see

A joined player's left panel shows only the Handbook tree: the readable pages of your campaign, in your order and nesting. Clicking one opens it in a panel over the map; Esc or the × closes it; the map, tokens and chat keep working beside it. Players cannot edit, rename, move or save a page — it is a copy that lives only while they are at your table. Pictures on a page reach them the same way play-map images do. Flowcharts render as you arranged them; diagram blocks render with an internet connection, in a stricter mode that ignores link and click directives.

Pages are for rules and reference, not for secrets: a page a player can read is a page every player at the table can read. Keep the twist in a planner or a room note.

How it works

Multiplayer is peer-to-peer: your app is the table, and your players' apps connect directly to yours. No accounts, no server, nothing to set up — everyone just needs Waypoint and an internet connection, and you keep the app open while playing.

Host a session

  1. Click 🌐 in the header and enter your display name — it’s required to host or join, and it’s how players know you at the table. In Settings › Profile you can set it once along with a color (your dot in the roster and on the maps) and a picture; they carry to every table.
  2. Players arrive at shows where joiners will land — leave it on Follow me or pick a specific map to pin the table there (handy for a staging area while you prep elsewhere). Once the campaign has had players, Player's last location is offered and is the default: each player comes back to the map they were last on and stays put until they travel or you summon them, so a split party picks up where it left off. New players start at appears under it and says where someone joining for the first time lands (Follow me, or a map of your choice).
  3. Press Host Session. A short room code appears — send it to your players however you like, then close the window: the session keeps running while you work, and the 🌐 button reopens it whenever you need the roster, pause, or summon.
  4. Players who join appear in the roster and see the arrival map. On Follow me, changing maps brings them along. Your planners, room notes, and character dossiers are never sent to them; Handbook pages you leave open to players are.

Give each player a token

  1. Select a character token on the play map.
  2. In the right sidebar, set Player Owner to that player (they must have joined at least once).
  3. From then on it's theirs: they can drag it, and when they join a session it's placed on your map automatically if it isn't already there.

Running the table

  • Hide secrets: right-click an item → Hide from Players. They see a grey cloud; you see a ghost with an eye. Reveal it the same way.
  • Handouts: the 📜 button holds pictures to show your players — a place, a face, a letter — each with a title and caption. A handout can also be text (a letter, a briefing, a recap). Show one to the table, or use Give to… to hand it to one player — the dropdown lists everyone registered in this campaign (by character), so you can prepare a handout for a player before the session: a connected player gets it at once, and anyone offline receives it the next time they join (the row shows who it is queued for). You can also attach a handout to a room (room Properties) so a player whose token enters that room sees it. Everything you show lands in that player's own Journal, on their machine, with their notes.
  • Party strip: the small tokens in the play map's top-right corner are your players' characters — highlighted when they are on the map you are viewing, dimmed when that player is not connected. Click one to jump to them (their map, centred, 150%); right-click one to summon that player, or everyone, to the table's map. When you're viewing a different map than the table's (you have it pinned elsewhere), the menu also offers Summon … to this map — for that one player (a scout ahead, the rest stay put) or for everyone (which moves the whole table here and re-pins it).
  • Split party: a player can double-click a door/stairs portal to move to that map alone. The roster shows where everyone is; Summon All pulls the whole party back to you, and the 📣 beside a name fetches just that player. The Maps list marks each map with a small face for the players on it — solid for anyone there now, dimmed for where a player was last seen — so the party's spread is visible at a glance.
  • Stream window: Settings ▶ Open Stream Window gives you a second window with only the play map, as players see it. Screen-share it for someone watching without the app; its corner menu follows your map, focuses one map, or focuses one character (switching maps with them and staying centred at 150%).
  • Player drawings: players have the pen and eraser too. Their marks ride above tokens without blocking them, and they can only erase their own.
  • Chat: click 💬 to message everyone, or pick one player to send a private whisper.
  • Pause the Table freezes players entirely — no token moves, no travel — while chat stays open; you can still move everything yourself. Use the ⏸️ button in the top Table bar (one click from either map while you host), the 🌐 panel, or the play-map right-click menu — they all stay in sync. To freeze just one player and leave the rest playing, right-click their token (or their party-strip chip) and pick Pause this player; their chip wears a blue ring until you resume them. A paused player sees a banner and can still chat.
  • Player token moves save into your campaign automatically — nothing is stored on their machines.
  • Handbook: the pages of the hosted campaign that are not marked GM only reach every player, who read them in a panel over the map. Turning a page GM only mid-session takes it away from them at once; your edits to an open page arrive as you type. Pages of your other campaigns never travel.
  • VTT features: the hosted campaign's settings (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features — elevation, posture, minimap, sound, dice, character sheets, visual effects, fog of war, and the VTT integration master) are the most your players see; a change mid-session reaches them at once. The 🌐 panel shows what the table runs with. Players may switch a feature off for themselves, never on. A player's own elevation or posture edit is accepted only while that feature is on for the campaign.
  • Sound: the 🎵 panel's ambient loop and cues play on every player's machine at your Master volume. Your uploaded files travel to each player over the connection (4 MB each, one at a time, the playing loop first); the bundled set never needs to (every install has it). A bundled sound you Hide leaves your lists and the table's list alike — players stop hearing it if it was playing — and Show hidden brings it back. A player can turn the table's sound off for themselves, never on when you have it off.
  • Character sheets: the campaign’s system reaches players with GM-only fields removed; a player gets their own character in full, other PCs’ Hover fields only, NPCs never. Their edits to fields you left player-editable are checked on your machine and saved into your campaign; you see them at once on the sheet and the hover card; a roll from a sheet is a table roll with the character’s values on the card. Help ▸ Character sheets.
  • Visual effects: the ✨ panel fires flashes, screen shake, color washes, bursts, weather and banners to the players on that map (and the stream window); a token can pulse from its menu, and the Blast tool gets a Boom. Off for the campaign hides it; a player can switch it off for themselves.
  • Fog of war: the 🌫 fog tool hides the map and shows each token only what it can see. Your machine works out every player’s vision and sends each of them only the cells and creatures they may see, so what is hidden never crosses the wire. On for the campaign by default; you run it for the whole table — there is no player switch. Help ▸ Play Map.
  • Dice: anyone at the table rolls with /roll in Table Chat or the 🎲 roller beside the message box; your machine makes every roll and everyone sees the same card, and every table roll lands in the session log under Dice. /gmroll is yours alone, a player's /gmroll comes to you only, and a whisper target makes your /roll private to that player. Help ▸ Dice has the syntax. Dice is a VTT feature: off for the campaign, nobody rolls.
  • One campaign per session: the table is the campaign you host. Switching campaigns in any way — the picker in the 🌐 panel or the sidebar, a search result, Ctrl + K, New or Delete Campaign, Import Replace, the tutorial — asks first and, on yes, ends the session for everyone before switching. Cancel changes nothing.

Who gets in

  • First-time joiners need your approval — a prompt asks "let them in?" before they see anything. Players you've hosted before walk straight in.
  • Optional session password: set one in the Host panel and the code alone isn't enough.
  • Kick: the × on a roster chip removes a player and keeps them out for the rest of the session. Denying an approval does the same.
If Waypoint crashes mid-session: relaunch it and press Host again within 15 minutes — you get the same room code back, players still reconnecting (they keep trying for about two minutes) reattach on their own, and anyone else rejoins with the code they already have.
The room code changes every session you end on purpose, so share it privately and yesterday's leak can't reach today's table. To end the session, open 🌐 and press Leave — players get a clear "session ended" notice.

If someone can't connect

Connections are direct between computers. Most home networks allow that; some do not — mobile data, many providers outside the US and EU, campus and hotel networks — and a player on one of those sits at "Connecting…" and then sees No route to the GM's table. Nothing on their side fixes it: the table needs a relay server, and only the GM sets one up. If a player drops out mid-session, their app retries automatically for a few minutes and reattaches when it can.

Setting up a relay server (GM only, once)

A relay (a TURN server) is a machine on the internet that both sides can reach; Waypoint sends the table's traffic through it when no direct path exists. The traffic stays end-to-end encrypted — the relay cannot read it. Waypoint does not ship with one, so you point it at yours:

  1. Get a relay. The quickest is a free account at metered.ca: after signing up, create an app (any unused name — it becomes a private subdomain and never appears in Waypoint) and open its TURN Server page. It lists relay addresses plus one username and one credential. Alternatives that work the same way: expressturn.com, or your own coturn server on a small VPS.
  2. Copy three things from that page: the address on port 443 with transport=tcp (it looks like turn:global.relay.metered.ca:443?transport=tcp — that form gets through the strictest networks), the username, and the credential.
  3. Paste them into ⚙️ Settings › Relay server and press Test Relay. "Working" means the relay answered. "Refused" is a wrong username or credential; "no answer" is a wrong address or port.
  4. Host again. The relay is used from the next time you host. Players change nothing — they press Join with the code as usual. A relayed join can take a few seconds longer.
Only the GM needs this. The settings stay on your machine (they are never written into the saves folder), so the credential does not travel with your campaign. If your firewall reports a "port scan" or "intruder" when players join, that is the direct-connection attempt; with a relay configured, Firewall-friendly connections appears under the relay box and routes everyone through the relay instead.
Free relay accounts carry a limited amount of traffic per month. Waypoint's table sync is small; images sent to a relayed player are the main cost.

Join your GM's table

  1. Get the room code from your GM (it looks like AB12CD), plus the session password if they set one.
  2. On the Welcome screen press Join a game (or the 🌐 button in the header any time) — set your name (required), your color and picture, enter the code (and password), and press Join. The Join screen shows the connection and drops you in when the GM’s table loads.
  3. Your first time at a table, the GM gets a prompt to let you in — you'll see "waiting for the GM" for a moment. After that you're recognized and go straight in.
  4. You'll land on whatever map the GM is showing, and follow along as they move the party.

  • Pan (Middle Click + Drag), zoom (Mouse Wheel), and use the 📏 Measure tool freely — your camera is your own.
  • Your token: if the GM assigned you one, just drag it. It snaps to the grid on hex maps. You can't move anyone else's.
  • Doors & stairs: double-click a portal marker to travel through on your own — the GM can summon the party back together.
  • Chat: click 💬 to talk to the table. Messages from the GM marked (private) are whispers only you can see.

  • Music: a song or playlist the GM set for a map starts on your machine as you enter it (it keeps playing if the next map uses the same one). The 🎶 note in the Table pill has your own music volume and mute, separate from Sound; the GM can take control of everyone's music for a moment.
  • Character sheets: right-click your token ▸ Sheet… to open your character’s sheet and fill in what the GM left editable; the GM’s Waypoint checks each value and keeps it. What you'll see

  • VTT features at the table: the GM's campaign decides which are on — token elevation, token posture, the minimap, sound, dice, character sheets, visual effects. ⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features shows the GM's setting for each and lets you switch one off for yourself (never on when the GM has it off); the choice is remembered for that table and never changes your own settings. It comes back only when the GM has changed the settings since you last saw it; a change mid-session is a short notice.

Where Waypoint is heading

Notes on features that are planned or partly in place, so you know what is a stopgap and what is coming. Plans change with play.

Build your own game system

  • A handbook for your campaign. Write the rules of your game inside Waypoint, and let players read them at the table.
  • Your rules, no coding. Set up the attributes, skills, resources, conditions and item types your game uses, and how they relate — a light homebrew or a granular system alike.
  • Character sheets, built by you. Lay out the sheet the way your game needs it. Every PC and NPC gets one in Waypoint, on its token, and players fill theirs in on the play map. Today's shadow-base.com attach is the first bridge of this kind, not the final shape.

Blasts thrown from the sheet

Rolling at the table

  • Type /roll 2d6 + 3 (or /r) in Table Chat, or press 🎲 beside the message box for the roller: a formula field (Enter rolls, ↑ and ↓ walk your last twenty rolls), quick buttons for d4 to d100, and Roll. A bare /roll is a d20.
  • One roll, one card for everyone. The GM's machine makes every roll at the table; each player's Waypoint replays the same dice and shows the same card — the formula, every die with what was kept, dropped, rerolled or exploded, and the total, or for a check the margin ("success by 3"). A lone d20 showing 20 or 1 is marked. Rolls work without a session too, as a card only you see.
  • Every table roll is written to the session log under Dice (on the GM's side).

The syntax

  • 2d6 + 3 — two six-sided dice plus three. d20, d% (percentile), 4dF (fudge dice).
  • 4d6kh3 keep the highest 3 · 2d20kl1 keep the lowest · dh1 / dl1 drop the highest / lowest.
  • 2d6! exploding (a top face rolls again and adds) · d20r1 reroll a 1 once · d6rr<=2 reroll while 2 or under.
  • 10d6cs>=5 count the dice showing 5 or more (successes).
  • d20 + 5 >= 16 a check: the card says success or failure and by how much · 3d6 <= 12 roll-under.
  • floor, ceil, round, abs, min, max, clamp, mod, if(cond, a, b), and / or / not; (Level)d6 and d(Faces) take a computed count or faces. Case does not matter; modifiers must touch the dice (4d6kh3, not 4d6 kh3).
  • Names such as STR or Skill.Stealth are the fields of the campaign’s system (Help ▸ Character sheets). Pick a character in the roller (your own; the GM any of the campaign’s) and the roll reads its sheet: d20 + Skill.Stealth shows Skill.Stealth (12) on the card, and the sheet’s roll buttons set the picker for you. A roll with names and no character is refused with a note. A GM’s roll that uses a GM-only value is kept private. A table roll is at most 300 characters and 200 dice.
  • Shift-click a sheet roll button (or a field’s 🎲) for a quick situational modifier: a small popover to add a bonus or penalty before you roll. It lands where it should — lifting the roll, or raising the target on a roll-under check — so a bonus always helps, and the card shows the adjusted formula. When the roll is a single die (a lone d20), the popover also offers Advantage / Disadvantage — roll it twice and keep the better or worse (2d20kh1 / 2d20kl1), which combines with the modifier. A plain click rolls straight away.

Private rolls

  • /gmroll (or /gr): the GM rolls for their eyes only (still logged); a player rolls to the GM — the player and the GM see it, the table does not. The roller's Private / To the GM only box does the same.
  • With a whisper target picked in the chat panel, the GM's /roll goes to that one player.

Sound and settings

  • The bundled Dice cue plays on your own machine on every roll while the roller's Sound box is ticked and Sound is on for you; nothing travels the wire for it.
  • Dice is a VTT feature (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features): off for the campaign, nobody rolls at that table and the roller is hidden. At a table a player may switch it off for themselves: their roller and dice sound go, the cards still show. Table chat clears when a session ends or a new one starts.
  • Visual effects (the ✨ button, a VTT feature): a panel of flashes, screen shake, color washes (darkness / red mist / blinding, held until you clear them), bursts you place on the map (boom, magic, smoke, sparks), weather that runs until you stop it (rain, snow, embers, haze) and text banners. Right-click a token for a Pulse; the 💥 Blast tool gains a Boom. The players on that map see it (and the stream window); a burst has a place, so a player on another map does not. Reduce motion (per machine, in Settings) skips shake and shortens flashes, and a hard flash guard prevents strobing.

The system

  • Press System in the Campaign pill (the sheet icon beside Rename) to write the rules of the campaign on screen, with no code: a list of fields, each with a key formulas use (STR, Skill.Stealth), a label, a kind, and what it stores or computes. Everything is saved with the campaign; Save in the editor keeps your changes, and players at a table get the visible fields at once.
  • Kinds: Number (an attribute: default, min, max, step) · Formula (computed from other fields, never stored: floor((STR - 10) / 2)) · Resource (a current value with a formula for its max, like HP: 10 + CONmod * Level; the default max starts it full) · Skill (stored ranks plus a base formula; its value is ranks + base; leave the base empty for absolute levels) · Toggle (on or off, a condition; if(Prone, -4, 0) works) · Text, Notes and Select (not numbers: a formula naming one gets an error).
  • Keys are one name to the formula engine: letters, digits, _ and dots, no spaces, never a function or reserved word (floor, and), never something that reads as dice (d6, dF), and the endings .max, .ranks, .cur, .base are reserved: HP.max is a resource’s max, Skill.Stealth.ranks the raw ranks.
  • Formulas use the dice engine’s syntax without dice: + - * / ^, floor ceil round abs min max clamp mod, if(cond, a, b), comparisons and and / or / not; up to 300 characters; a formula may name other fields, in any order, but not in a loop. Dice belong in rolls only — a die inside a field’s definition is refused, because a sheet must not re-roll every time it is drawn.
  • Errors show under the field as you type, with a caret under the offending place; Compute with defaults at the foot of the Fields tab evaluates every formula so you can see the numbers.
  • Visible to players or GM only per field: a GM-only field and its value never leave your machine, and a visible formula that names one shows an error to players (the editor warns you). Player may edit or GM edits decides who changes the value at the table once sheets exist; formulas are never edited. Hover marks fields for the token’s hover card and the party strip.
  • Rolls are named formulas with dice (d20 + STRmod, 3d6 <= Skill.Stealth) that become buttons on the sheet; a field can carry its own roll too. A roll fires through the table’s dice engine and lands in chat like any other roll. Tick Initiative on the roll the combat roster should use.
  • Layout (the System editor’s Layout tab) arranges the sheet: sections of 1–4 columns holding your fields, roll buttons, headings, a divider or the portrait, dragged into place. For a richer sheet, add Tabs and give each section a tab — the sheet then shows a tab strip (Info / Combat / …). A section can be made collapsible (the reader folds it away), show a count in its header (any field’s value, such as a points total), and be nested under another as a sub-section. Give a section its own colors (an accent, a panel background and a border) for a card look, and mark a numeric field as a stat tile (a boxed value with a small label, for ability-score grids) — both optional. No tabs means one stacked page, and the automatic layout is always there to start from — so simple sheets stay simple.
  • Start from… replaces the system with Basic d20 (six attributes with modifiers, HP, AC, skills with d20 rolls, conditions) or Basic 3d6 (ST/DX/IQ/HT, HP/FP, skills as levels with roll-under checks) or a blank; Export writes a .wpsystem.json you can Import into another campaign or share.
  • Character sheets is a VTT feature (⚙ Settings ▸ VTT features): off for the campaign, the system stays yours to edit but nothing of it shows at the table; a player who turns it off for themselves loses the sheet panel and the hover lines, their token still moves.

Items and blasts from the sheet

  • The System editor’s Items tab is the campaign’s item library — weapons, gear and explosives. Each item has a name, a category, an optional blast area (a radius in feet), a damage roll (dice allowed: 3d6 + STRmod), a cost formula, an icon and a Visible / GM only flag. GM-only items and all item formulas never leave your machine.
  • A character carries items through an Item list field (add it in Fields, place it on the sheet in Layout): the sheet then shows an inventory with a qty −/+, a remove, and an + Add item… picker of the library. Tick Rich table on that field to show it as a table instead of a plain list — choose the columns (category, cost, damage, area), an expandable notes row, category chips and a totals footer; the sheet widens to fit it, and off it stays the plain list. A player manages their own; the host checks every change.
  • An item with a blast area gets a 💥 Throw button: press it, then click the map. The blast lands attributed to the character and is shared with everyone on that map. (The toolbar 💥 stays a personal GM quick-tool.)
  • Combat settings (top of the Items tab): Blast automation — Full auto rolls the damage and applies it to every token in range, Roll to chat rolls but leaves applying to you, Measure only just shows the area. Who rolls — the character’s owner or always the GM. Damage subtracts from — the resource (HP) full-auto reduces. Undo a full-auto throw with ↩ Undo last throw in the 💥 menu.

Characters and their sheets

  • A character belongs to the campaign, not to a token: the editor’s Characters tab creates them (name, the player who plays them, NPC, a portrait) and any number of tokens can point at one — a PC with tokens on two maps has one HP total. Pick the character in a token’s Properties (Character (sheet); New character from this token makes one named after it), or right-click a token ▸ New character sheet…. Duplicating, pasting or dropping several copies from the Cast makes separate creatures; a single Cast drop keeps the link.
  • Ownership: the character’s player is written onto every token that points at it, so moves, arrival and the party strip keep working as before; setting a token’s owner in Properties updates the character too. An NPC has no player and never reaches players; an unassigned character is yours alone until you pick a player.
  • The sheet panel: right-click a token ▸ Sheet…, or Open sheet from Properties or the Characters tab. It floats over the play map (drag its head, position remembered), shows the sections of the layout — numbers and skills with their computed totals, resources as a bar with − and +, toggles, text, notes, selects, formulas read-only with the breakdown as a tooltip — and saves every change at once. ↶ puts back the last value you changed. A GM-only field is marked; players never see it.
  • Players open their own character’s sheet (right-click their token ▸ Sheet…) and edit the fields you left Player may edit; your Waypoint checks every value (range, step, options, a resource’s max) before it lands, and the sheet shows the change while it waits. Of other PCs a player sees only the Hover fields (the hover card and the party strip); NPCs stay with you. An edit that arrives too fast or breaks a rule is refused with a note and put back.
  • Hover: fields marked Hover show under the token’s name on the hover card and in the party strip’s tooltip (HP 7 / 14 · Prone).
  • Rolls from the sheet: the roll buttons (a system roll, or a field’s own 🎲 beside its label) roll at the table like /roll, with the character’s values filled in; the card shows the label, the character and every name with the value it used, so everyone sees the same numbers without the sheet. Players roll from their own sheet; the GM from any. In the combat roster, 🎲 beside a row rolls initiative from the system’s Initiative roll.
  • Layout: the editor’s Layout tab arranges the sheet in sections of 1–4 columns: add a field, a roll button, a heading, a divider or the portrait to a section, set each to one column or the full row, drag rows to reorder or move them between sections, and watch the preview with a chosen character’s values. Without a layout the sheet arranges itself (one section per kind, then the rolls); Start from the automatic layout copies that as a starting point. A field left off the sheet stays defined (rollable, on the hover card). Save keeps the layout with the system, and players’ sheets follow at once.
  • From a ShadowBase sheet: a token with a ShadowBase JSON attached (Properties ▸ ShadowBase Sheet) gets Character from this sheet: a one-time copy of its attributes, resources and skills into a campaign character, by the system’s matching keys (ST or STR, DX or DEX, HT or CON, IQ or INT, HP, FP, Will, Per, Dodge, Parry; skills by name, levels converted to ranks over the base). The ShadowBase sheet stays attached and separate; copy again any time with Copy the sheet’s values into the character. The party strip’s right-click menu offers Sheet… and Characters… too, and the Tutorial campaign carries a small system with Bren’s sheet to try.
