Help & Tutorials

What Waypoint is

Waypoint organizes a tabletop campaign into Maps (locations with two faces: a node-based Data Map for your notes and a freeform Play Map for the battle map) and Planners (document pages for session plans). Everything lives in the left sidebar.

Your first map

  1. Press + next to Maps in the left sidebar and name it.
  2. The Properties sidebar on the right opens when you select a room or an item and closes when the selection clears; the ▶ button on its edge opens or closes it by hand.
  3. Use the Data Map / Play Map switch at the top to flip between the two faces of the same location.
  4. Drag one map onto another in the sidebar to nest it inside (world → city → building). A breadcrumb appears up top so you always know where you are.

Getting around fast

  • Ctrl + K opens quick-jump: type any map, planner, or room name and hit Enter to go straight there.
  • The minimap (bottom-right corner) shows where your content is — click anywhere on it to fly the camera there.
  • Drag the thin gutters beside either sidebar to resize the panels.

Saving & backups

  • Everything autosaves to saves/data.json a moment after each change — there is no save button to forget.
  • To back up or move your campaign, copy the saves folder. That's the whole thing.
  • Export As makes shareable files at any scope: just the open map or planner, all maps / play maps / planners in the campaign, or everything. Exports that use images arrive as a .zip with the pictures bundled in; Import accepts those zips (or plain .json) and merges by id or replaces.
Waypoint works fully offline. Internet is only needed for Multiplayer and for diagram blocks in Planners.

Tools

  • ➚ Select / Move (default) — click rooms to edit them, drag to move, drag a box to select several.
  • ✋ Pan — drag anywhere to move the map itself; rooms never move while it's active. (Middle-click drag pans in any tool.)
  • ↔ Link Mode — lit gold while active; click two rooms to connect them. Pick another tool to leave it.
  • 🗑 Clear the board — removes every room and link on this map after a confirmation (Undo brings them back).

Rooms (nodes)

  1. Click ➕ Add Room, then drag the node where you want it.
  2. Select it and fill in the right sidebar: name, category color, notes, and the characters found there.
  3. Set Room Image does double duty: the picture shows on hover, and Waypoint builds a child map with that image as the scene, linking the node to it as a portal. (Nodes that already lead somewhere just get the picture.)
  4. Double Click a map or planner name in the sidebar to rename it.
  5. Categories are per-map — add your own under Categories. Press ☆ on one (or Save all to library) and any other map can add it from the Category Library with the same name and color.
  6. + Add under Characters also drops a stand-in token on the play map (initials in a circle); set a portrait and the token wears it, rename the character and the token follows.

Connections

  1. Click ↔ Link Mode, then click two rooms one after the other to connect them.
  2. Still in Link Mode, click the small × on a line to remove it.

Linked maps (doors, stairs, warps)

  1. Select a room and set its Linked Map to another map, plus an icon (door, stairs, gate…).
  2. Double Click the room — or any play map item tied to it — to travel to that map. Use the breadcrumb to climb back out. In multiplayer, a player travels by dropping their token on the portal item (double-click works too), and the GM can send a player through by dropping that player's token there.

GM notes on the play map

Select a play map item, link it to a room in the right sidebar, and press Generate GM Notes to build a styled note card from that room's notes and characters. Hover a linked item to preview the room's info.

Placing things

  • Pick a shape or T text from the Shapes menu, then click the board to drop it there — or drag to draw exactly the box it should fill (hexagons resize like any other shape). Esc cancels. The New shape size switch in that menu picks Free (default size on click, exact box on drag) or Grid cell (every new shape arrives exactly one cell in size and seats into the cell — handy for hex tokens and tiles). 🖼️ adds an image, or just drag an image file from your computer onto the board.
  • Text boxes: Double Click to edit the words; the color swatch (toolbar or Properties) sets the text color.
  • 🗄️ Image Library shows every image already saved in this install — click one to place it.
  • Ctrl + C / Ctrl + X / Ctrl + V copy, cut, and paste selected items; pasting a screenshot or copied image from outside the app drops it at your cursor.
  • The New item opacity slider in the Shapes and Pen menus sets the transparency new items are created with.
  • Click to select; drag the corner handle to resize (hold Shift — or turn on Lock proportions in the selection toolbar — to keep the width/height ratio; resizing one member of a group scales the whole group); drag the blue dot to rotate. Arrow keys nudge the selection (Shift for bigger steps).
  • ✏️ Draw sketches freehand (click it again for color, stroke size, straight-line mode, and opacity). 🧽 Eraser rubs out only what its circle passes over — the rest of the stroke stays. Click the eraser again for its size and mode: Precise trims strokes exactly to the circle, Segments removes whole point-to-point pieces.
  • 🎯 Center menu: jump to the canvas center, to all items, or Center on Character — your own token in multiplayer, or the selected one as GM.
  • Drop a ⚡ Trigger Zone and give it an Event Message — it fires when a character token is dragged inside. The button picks a shape to fit the grid (hex on hex maps, square on square grids); change it any time under Trigger Shape.
  • Any item can be a portal: Properties → Portal to Map. On a nested map the parent is offered first (⬆ Back to…), so a way home takes one click — no node needed.

The selection toolbar

A small toolbar floats above whatever you select: color, layer up/down, group/ungroup (🔗 — lit gold when the selection is a group), lock proportions (the dashed box with a diagonal arrow — a padlock appears on it when on), lock (the padlock shows the current state: closed = locked), player visibility, duplicate (⧉ — a full copy with every setting and attached data; also Ctrl + D, the right-click menu, and Properties), delete. Select several items and right-click for Align and Space (distribute) buttons.

Who can see this? Every item is either 👁 Visible to players or 🚫 Hidden from players. The big box at the top of the Properties panel shows which, and the toolbar's eye turns red when hidden. Hidden items show to you dimmed; players at your table see nothing at all. Use it for secret doors, unrevealed enemies, and GM-only notes.

The right-click menu

  • Lock / Unlock — locked items can't be moved or resized (they dim slightly).
  • Hide from Players / Show to Players — in multiplayer, hidden items appear to players as a grey cloud; you see a ghost with an eye.
  • Show Above Grid — lifts an item above the grid lines (text does this automatically).
  • Bring to Front / Forward / Backward / Send to Back — five stacking layers for building scenes.
  • Opacity slider — adjust transparency of anything already on the board.
  • Select several items (drag a box around them) to Group them or Merge Drawings.
Character tokens: any shape or image with Is Character set in the right sidebar becomes a token with a name and stats tooltip — and can be handed to a player in multiplayer. Tokens pair with shadow-base.com character sheets: the 🤵 Import Character toolbar button turns a site JSON into a ready token (portrait as art, sheet attached), the inspector's View opens the full read-only sheet (stats, traits, skills, inventory), and Export JSON writes a site-ready file back out — no sheet attached still exports a starter file.

Grids

  1. Click the grid button in the play map toolbar to cycle Off → Square → Hex. Hex cells are flat-top (a flat side faces up). The choice is remembered per map.
  2. Grids draw on top of your art so cells stay visible; text lifts above the grid automatically, and any other item can via right-click → Show Above Grid.
  3. Turn on 🧲 Snap and tokens seat themselves into cells as you drop them. On hex maps, character tokens always center into cells, and freehand strokes follow cell corners even on fast swipes.
  4. Tokens follow rooms: link a floor shape to a room (Properties → Link Node) and any character token dropped onto it moves into that room's character list automatically — the data map always knows who is where.
  5. Snap To (click the Snap button again) picks what a drag aligns to: Grid — cells only, the most predictable; Items — neighbours only, for tidy rows; Both — neighbours first, grid for open-space drops. Remembered per computer.
  6. Tokens turn in cell-side steps while a grid is on: 4 facings on a square grid, 6 on a hex grid (one per face — hex cells are flat-top, so straight up is a face). Click a selected token's arrow to turn it one facing clockwise (Shift-click = counter-clockwise), or drag the arrow to aim it; the blue rotate dot works too. Hold Shift while turning for free 15° steps. Properties → Turning chooses whether the art turns with the arrow or the arrow alone moves around an upright picture. With no grid they turn freely; applying a grid turns every token to its nearest facing. A small gold arrow marks each token's front and always points across a cell face at a neighbouring cell — pick which side of the art that is under Properties → Front Side. Players can turn their own token.
  7. Token condition: right-click a token (or use Properties → Condition) for Alive, Incapacitated (red X over the token) or Dead (skull, art darkened). Players see it too.
  8. In Items or Both mode, Snap makes item edges magnetic: drag an image next to another and it glues flush and falls into line automatically — rows and columns without pixel-hunting. Item-to-item snaps win over the grid; drop something in open space and the grid takes it.
  9. Resizing matches sizes too (Snap on or off): pull a corner near another item's width or height and it pauses there with a ↔ / ↕ badge — ease past to break free, release to keep the match. With proportions locked, the other side follows.
  10. ⊞ Fit to Grid (selection toolbar or Properties) sizes the selection to whole cells and seats it — tokens and hex shapes become exactly one hex cell.

Measuring

  1. Pick the 📏 Measure tool and drag across the map to place a ruler; click the tool again for options.
  2. Choose Imperial or Metric. By default a hex cell is 1 yard across and a square is 5 feet.
  3. For huge maps (space battles!), set Map Scale in the measure options — e.g. one cell = 100 yd or 2 km. Each map keeps its own scale.
  4. Click any placed ruler to remove it (it turns red on hover); Clear rulers in the measure options removes them all.

Coordinate rulers & minimap

The number strips along the top and left edges are coordinate rulers. Turn them off (or back on) in ⚙ Settings → Table, alongside the minimap toggle and a Grid opacity slider — that slider is yours alone, so in multiplayer every player can fade the grid to taste without changing anyone else's view. Zoom steps are 5% per click or Mouse Wheel tick (the wheel zooms toward the pointer; hold Shift to scroll instead).

Building pages

  1. Press + next to Planners and open your new page.
  2. Use the planner toolbar to add blocks: headings, prose, callouts, tables, and flowcharts. Blocks can be reordered and edited in place.
  3. Planners nest under planners just like maps — keep one parent per module with a child page per scene.
Diagram blocks render with an internet connection. In multiplayer, planners are never sent to players — they are yours alone.

How it works

Multiplayer is peer-to-peer: your app is the table, and your players' apps connect directly to yours. No accounts, no server, nothing to set up — everyone just needs Waypoint and an internet connection, and you keep the app open while playing.

Host a session

  1. Click 🌐 in the header and enter your display name.
  2. Players arrive at shows where joiners will land — leave it on Follow me or pick a specific map to pin the table there (handy for a staging area while you prep elsewhere).
  3. Press Host Session. A short room code appears — send it to your players however you like, then close the window: the session keeps running while you work, and the 🌐 button reopens it whenever you need the roster, pause, or summon.
  4. Players who join appear in the roster and see the arrival map. On Follow me, changing maps brings them along. Your planners, room notes, and character dossiers are never sent to them.

Give each player a token

  1. Select a character token on the play map.
  2. In the right sidebar, set Player Owner to that player (they must have joined at least once).
  3. From then on it's theirs: they can drag it, and when they join a session it's placed on your map automatically if it isn't already there.

Running the table

  • Hide secrets: right-click an item → Hide from Players. They see a grey cloud; you see a ghost with an eye. Reveal it the same way.
  • Handouts: the 📜 button holds pictures to show your players — a place, a face, a letter — each with a title and caption. A handout can also be text (a letter, a briefing, a recap). Show one to the table or to one player, or attach it to a room (room Properties) so a player whose token enters that room sees it. Everything you show lands in that player's own Journal, on their machine, with their notes.
  • Party strip: the small tokens in the play map's top-right corner are your players' characters — highlighted when they are on the map you are viewing, dimmed when that player is not connected. Click one to jump to them (their map, centred, 150%); right-click one to summon that player, or everyone, to the table's map.
  • Split party: a player can double-click a door/stairs portal to move to that map alone. The roster shows where everyone is; Summon All pulls the whole party back to you, and the 📣 beside a name fetches just that player.
  • Stream window: Settings ▶ Open Stream Window gives you a second window with only the play map, as players see it. Screen-share it for someone watching without the app; its corner menu follows your map, focuses one map, or focuses one character (switching maps with them and staying centred at 150%).
  • Player drawings: players have the pen and eraser too. Their marks ride above tokens without blocking them, and they can only erase their own.
  • Chat: click 💬 to message everyone, or pick one player to send a private whisper.
  • Pause the Table (in the 🌐 panel while hosting) freezes players entirely — no token moves, no travel — while chat stays open. Press it again to resume. You can still move everything yourself while paused.
  • Player token moves save into your campaign automatically — nothing is stored on their machines.

Who gets in

  • First-time joiners need your approval — a prompt asks "let them in?" before they see anything. Players you've hosted before walk straight in.
  • Optional session password: set one in the Host panel and the code alone isn't enough.
  • Kick: the × on a roster chip removes a player and keeps them out for the rest of the session. Denying an approval does the same.
If Waypoint crashes mid-session: relaunch it and press Host again within 15 minutes — you get the same room code back, players still reconnecting (they keep trying for about two minutes) reattach on their own, and anyone else rejoins with the code they already have.
The room code changes every session you end on purpose, so share it privately and yesterday's leak can't reach today's table. To end the session, open 🌐 and press Leave — players get a clear "session ended" notice.

If someone can't connect

Connections are direct between computers. Most home networks allow that; some do not — mobile data, many providers outside the US and EU, campus and hotel networks — and a player on one of those sits at "Connecting…" and then sees No route to the GM's table. Nothing on their side fixes it: the table needs a relay server, and only the GM sets one up. If a player drops out mid-session, their app retries automatically for a few minutes and reattaches when it can.

Setting up a relay server (GM only, once)

A relay (a TURN server) is a machine on the internet that both sides can reach; Waypoint sends the table's traffic through it when no direct path exists. The traffic stays end-to-end encrypted — the relay cannot read it. Waypoint does not ship with one, so you point it at yours:

  1. Get a relay. The quickest is a free account at metered.ca: after signing up, create an app (any unused name — it becomes a private subdomain and never appears in Waypoint) and open its TURN Server page. It lists relay addresses plus one username and one credential. Alternatives that work the same way: expressturn.com, or your own coturn server on a small VPS.
  2. Copy three things from that page: the address on port 443 with transport=tcp (it looks like turn:global.relay.metered.ca:443?transport=tcp — that form gets through the strictest networks), the username, and the credential.
  3. Paste them into ⚙️ Settings › Relay server and press Test Relay. "Working" means the relay answered. "Refused" is a wrong username or credential; "no answer" is a wrong address or port.
  4. Host again. The relay is used from the next time you host. Players change nothing — they press Join with the code as usual. A relayed join can take a few seconds longer.
Only the GM needs this. The settings stay on your machine (they are never written into the saves folder), so the credential does not travel with your campaign. If your firewall reports a "port scan" or "intruder" when players join, that is the direct-connection attempt; with a relay configured, Firewall-friendly connections appears under the relay box and routes everyone through the relay instead.
Free relay accounts carry a limited amount of traffic per month. Waypoint's table sync is small; images sent to a relayed player are the main cost.

Join your GM's table

  1. Get the room code from your GM (it looks like AB12CD), plus the session password if they set one.
  2. Click 🌐 in the header, type your name, enter the code (and password), and press Join.
  3. Your first time at a table, the GM gets a prompt to let you in — you'll see "waiting for the GM" for a moment. After that you're recognized and go straight in.
  4. You'll land on whatever map the GM is showing, and follow along as they move the party.

What you can do

  • Pan (Middle Click + Drag), zoom (Mouse Wheel), and use the 📏 Measure tool freely — your camera is your own.
  • Your token: if the GM assigned you one, just drag it. It snaps to the grid on hex maps. You can't move anyone else's.
  • Doors & stairs: double-click a portal marker to travel through on your own — the GM can summon the party back together.
  • Chat: click 💬 to talk to the table. Messages from the GM marked (private) are whispers only you can see.

What you'll see

  • Grey cloud boxes are things the GM hasn't revealed yet.
  • If a banner says the GM paused the table, tokens and doors are frozen until they resume — chat stays open.
  • Other players' tokens only show on the map they're actually on.
  • Journal (📖): everything a GM has shown you, saved on this computer with its caption. Add notes under each handout, or press + Note to write your own pages in a campaign's section; it all works with no session running.
  • Targeting: click a character token that isn't yours to mark it as your target — everyone sees a small copy of your own token on it (several targeters line up in the middle and wrap into rows). Click it again, or press Esc, to clear. One target at a time.
  • Editing tools are the GM's — your token moves are saved on their side, and nothing is stored on your machine.
Dropped out? Just join again with the same code. To leave on purpose, open 🌐 and press Leave — your own campaigns come right back.

Mouse

  • Middle Click + Drag   Pan
  • Mouse Wheel   Zoom toward the pointer, 5% a tick (Shift + Wheel scrolls; click the zoom % to type one)
  • Double Click   Edit a text block / travel through a portal
  • Right Click   Item menu (lock, hide, layers, opacity, above-grid)
  • Click + Drag on empty board   Box-select multiple items

Keyboard

  • Ctrl + Z / Ctrl + Y   Undo / Redo
  • Ctrl + C / Ctrl + X / Ctrl + V   Copy / cut / paste play map items (or paste an image from outside)
  • Ctrl + K   Quick-jump to any map, planner, or room
  • Arrow Keys   Nudge the selection 1px (Shift = 10px)
  • Delete or Backspace   Delete the selected item

Toolbar

  • 🎯 Center   Reset the camera (to the canvas, or to fit your items)
  • 🧲 Snap   Toggle grid snapping (remembered between launches)
